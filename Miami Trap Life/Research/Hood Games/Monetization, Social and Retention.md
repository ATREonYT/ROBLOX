# Monetization, social features and what keeps players coming back

Back to [[00 Hood Games Index]]. Our money rules are in `Build Playbooks/21 Money and Retention at Studio Level.md` (repo root) and `Config/Monetization.lua`. This note is what the hood games actually sell and why players return.

## 1. What hood games sell

| Type | Examples in the genre | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Cash packs (developer products) | Da Hood has 132 products. Urban Reload sells R$1,499 = $1M up to R$29,999 = $100M | The biggest earner in the genre and the biggest source of "pay to win" complaints |
| Multipliers | 2x cash, 2x XP, as passes and as timed boosts | Standard. We already have these |
| Gun and item passes | The "Switch" in Tha Bronx 3, guns with a Robux price next to the cash price (OPPS) | Sells the dream item to people who won't grind |
| Cases and skins | Weapon cases ($5k–30k in-game cash in Urban Reload), skin cases with posted odds (BlockSpin) | Odds must be shown |
| Convenience | Extra safe slots (Urban Reload "+10 Safe"), bigger bags, car spawn anywhere | Low drama, players like these |
| VIP / private servers | Most games | Cheap and well liked |
| Revenge or second chance | OPPS "Get Revenge?" for Robux on the death screen | Players hate it. Don't copy |

Lessons:
- **The fair games rate better.** Hood Rivals has only 5 passes and 90% likes. Da Hood has 146 things to buy and 70.5%.
- **Sell time and looks, not power.** Boosts, skins, cars, convenience. Avoid selling guns that beat skill.
- **One store, two currencies is confusing.** If a gun has a Robux price, show the cash price next to it so it doesn't read as pay-only.
- Kid-safe and policy rules from the playbook apply: posted odds, no fake timers, no pressure screens.

## 2. Social features
- **Crews / gangs / groups:** a name tag over the head, crew colour, crew chat, shared territory or base, splitting heist payouts. In South Bronx groups are the main identity.
- **Sending money** through the phone (South Bronx). Gifting guns and items.
- **Trading** on a player black market (Tha Bronx 3 for the Switch).
- **Roleplay identity:** an RP name, an ID card, a job title, a mask that hides who you are.
- **Discord as the second half of the game:** patch notes, codes, reports of unfair kills (Streetz War 2 shows a player ID after you die for that reason), trading.
- **Rules against random killing (RDM) and fail-RP** in the roleplay-first games, with bans.

## 3. Live ops
- Seasonal events (winter, Halloween, February in Tha Bronx 3; Halloween ghost hunts in Urban Reload) with a code each time.
- **Codes** for small rewards (cash, skill points), redeemed in the menu. Codes are posted on Discord and in video titles, which is free marketing.
- Weekly or bi-weekly updates during the launch window. Games that stop updating lose most players within a couple of months (see the peaks in [[Game Profiles]]).
- Re-releases and "overhauls" with a new UI bring players back (Tha Bronx 3's overhaul came with a new UI and new job pay).

## 4. Why the popular ones are sticky
1. **A ladder with visible next steps.** You always know the next item you're saving for.
2. **Risk you choose.** Crime is optional and pays more, so playing safe or risky is a personal style.
3. **Other players are the content.** Chases, robberies, crews and rivalries make every server different.
4. **Status you can see.** Cars, drip, guns and a crew tag show other people how far you've come.
5. **Something to lose.** Dirty cash and gear on you can be taken, which makes getting home safe feel good.
6. **Identity.** An RP name and a crew make you somebody in the server.
7. **Short sessions still pay.** A 10-minute session can do one robbery or one job run and feel worthwhile.
8. **Constant small updates** and codes give a reason to check back.

## 5. Why players leave (complaints across the genre)
- Cheaters and exploiters (Da Hood above all).
- Being killed over and over at spawn by stronger players.
- Pay to win.
- Nothing to do after the first hour if you don't want to fight.
- Confusing onboarding: not knowing where jobs are, what to buy, or how to earn (see `Onboarding Funnels and Wayfinding.md`).
- Laggy, cluttered UI on mobile.
