# Economy, jobs and crime in hood games

Back to [[00 Hood Games Index]]. This is the reference for how money moves in the genre. The design proposals for our own game are in [[What Makes Hood RP Games Fun]] §5 and `Hood Jobs - Design.md`; this note collects the patterns and numbers they are built on.

## 1. The money ladder (every top game has one)

Every hood game sorts its money methods from **safe and slow** to **risky and fast**. The steps are almost the same everywhere:

| Rung | Typical method | Pay (Tha Bronx 3 numbers) | Risk | What it needs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Starting cash | $1,000 | none | none |
| 1 | Menial job (mop, boxes, pizza, cashier) | ~$45 an item | none | nothing, walk in |
| 2 | Skilled job that levels up (construction) | ~$1,200 a block at job level 12 | none | time |
| 3 | Petty crime (looting, registers, ATMs, trap houses) | burst of dirty cash | other players | a bag to carry more |
| 4 | Store or studio robbery | $3,000 / $7,500 / $16,000 by tier | police and players | a gun, cooldowns |
| 5 | Scams (cards, laptops) | varies | police | bought tools, know-how |
| 6 | Bank or vault heist | the highest ceiling | everyone | C4, mask, duffel, a crew |
| 7 | Player houses and safes | whatever they own | the owner | buy access |

Rules the good games follow:
- **Each rung pays clearly more per minute than the one below**, roughly 3-10x, or nobody climbs.
- **Each rung needs one bought item** (a bag, a gun, a mask, C4). That item is the reason to spend money, and buying it is the "I'm moving up" moment.
- **Crime has cooldowns**, so you rotate methods (the Tha Bronx 3 wiki literally says "rotate studio robbery with construction").
- **A legal path always exists** so a careful player can still get rich slowly.

## 2. Dirty money and washing it
- Crime pays **dirty money**. You **wash** it at a laundromat (Tha Bronx 3) or similar front before you can bank it or buy top-tier gear.
- Some shady shops (gun store, black market) accept dirty cash directly. That keeps the criminal path playable without the wash step every time.
- Dirty money **on you** can be lost: dropped on death, taken by police, stolen. Money in the bank is safe (Streetz War 2 says this outright).
- **Why it works:** it creates the walk (or drive) from the crime to the laundromat to the ATM. That trip is where chases, ambushes and stories happen.

## 3. Jobs
- **Menial jobs exist mainly as a tutorial and a floor.** Players and guides call them "slow" and "skip it" once they know better. The good ones are short minigames, not waiting.
- **One job that levels up** (construction in Tha Bronx 3) is the steady earner for players who don't want risk. Pay scales hard with job level (×20 or more from level 1 to 12).
- **Holding more than one job** pays more in South Bronx, which rewards knowing the map.
- **Jobs as cover.** In roleplay games a job is also an alibi and a social place: the cashier sees everyone.

## 4. Crime loops
- **Looting** (trap houses, registers, ATMs): instant, solo, low payout, PvP risk. The "first crime" in nearly every game.
- **Robbery with stages:** get the tool, break in (hold E to drill or bag), grab, escape. Tiers by difficulty and payout. Cooldown between runs.
- **Heists need a crew:** C4 for the vault, one person drills, one watches, one drives. This is what makes crews necessary.
- **Scams:** buy fake cards or a laptop, use ATMs, cash out at the bank. Quiet, no shooting, a different kind of player.
- **House robbery:** player houses with TVs and safes, which puts other players' stuff at stake.
- **What sells the moment:** a hold-to-do progress bar, a loud alarm, a red "wanted" state, and a timer that pushes you to leave.

## 5. Police and heat
- Real-player police teams (NYPD in Tha Bronx 3, cops in Da Hood and the Chicago games) with cuffs and jail. NPC police exist in some games for empty servers.
- A heat or wanted state after crime, visible on the HUD. Cops get a marker on the crime.
- Jail as a timer you can wait out, pay out of, or be broken out of by your crew.

## 6. Survival stats
- **Hunger and energy (or stamina)** in Tha Bronx 3 change how far you can sprint and how well you fight. You refill them with cheap food you cook, coffee, or sleep.
- They add a reason to own a place (a bed), to visit shops, and to carry food. They should never kill you outright; they just make you weaker.
- **For Hood Life:** if we add them, keep it to **one** bar (energy) and make refilling it a nice moment (the corner store, a coffee at the mart), not a chore.

## 7. Gear ladder and the dream item
- Bags (carry more loot), masks (hide your name, needed for heists), guns by tier, armor, attachments, and one **dream item** that everyone talks about: the "Switch" (full-auto kit) in Tha Bronx 3, rare gun skins in Urban Reload and BlockSpin.
- The dream item is sold three ways at once: a long grind for cash, a Robux pass, and player trading. That gives it value for everyone.

## 8. What this means for Hood Life
1. Keep our legal jobs as rungs 1-2, and make one of them level up hard (the freight or delivery job is the best candidate).
2. Build rungs 3-6 as **repeatable** crimes with cooldowns, each gated by one item from a shop.
3. Dirty cash on you, a wash spot (a Miami-style laundromat or a car wash front), and bank money that is safe. This one rule gives the map its traffic.
4. One dream item for the criminal path and one for the legal path (a car).
5. All crime language stays in our kid-safe wording (see the game's `CLAUDE.md`): "dropped", "hustle", "lick", no gore.
