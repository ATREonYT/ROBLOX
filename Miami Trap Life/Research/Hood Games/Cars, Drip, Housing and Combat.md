# Cars, drip, housing and combat in hood games

Back to [[00 Hood Games Index]]. What players spend their money on, and how fights work. Our own specs: `Gun Mechanics.md`, `Movement and Game Feel.md`, `Research/Benchmarks/Benchmark Gap List.md` waves 1, 3 and 4.

## 1. Cars
- **A ladder, not a gift.** The cheap first ride is part of the joke and the motivation: a van at the hill dealership (Tha Bronx 3), a mobility scooter (BlockSpin), a beater hatchback (Urban Reload). The supercar or the big SUV is the dream.
- **Dealership UI** is a showroom: the car turns on a plinth, with name, price, top speed, and a big Buy button. Test drives are common.
- **Spawning:** most games spawn your car from a menu or a hotbar slot (Urban Reload puts it in slot 5) instead of making you walk to a garage.
- **Garage customisation** is the long-term money sink: paint (gloss, matte, metallic), rims, tint, neon underglow, headlight colour, spoilers, suspension height, horns, plates. Urban Reload prices neons at $150,000 and headlights at $100,000.
- **Car feel that players notice:** handbrake drifts with tyre smoke, a horn, lock/unlock, a radio, and being able to run people over (with damage to the car).
- **For Hood Life:** we hand out the Urus-style SUV too early. Start on a bike or a beater, make the SUV the goal, and put paint, rims and tint in a garage app on the phone.

## 2. Drip (clothes and looks)
- Clothing stores with real-brand parodies (Streetz War 2 has Zara- and JD-style shops, Tha Bronx 3 has a "Drip Store"). Masks, glasses, chains, hats, bags.
- Masks are both style and function: they hide your name and they are needed for heists.
- Outfit saving and quick-change from the menu (Urban Reload has a "Wardrobe" button on the HUD).
- Character creators with an RP name (South Bronx locks the name in, so it means something).
- **For Hood Life:** a clothing store in the mart strip with three or four racks, a mask rack for crime, and an outfit slot or two in the phone.

## 3. Housing
- Apartments to rent or buy (Streetz War 2), custom crib interiors (Philly Streetz 2), houses other players can break into (Tha Bronx 3).
- A home gives you: a bed (energy), a safe (protected money or items), a place to show off, and something to defend.
- **For Hood Life:** our flat already has the story and the power bill. Add a safe, a bed that refills energy, and a few buyable decor items, then let crew members visit.

## 4. Combat
- **Knock then finish** (Da Hood's stomp, Urban Reload's "you must finish them to get paid"). Gives a rescue window, a revive, and a moment of drama. We already have the downed state.
- **Loot on death:** weapons and dirty cash drop, so a fight has stakes. Protected slots (Urban Reload sells extra safe slots) let players keep their favourite.
- **Gun shop UI:** a grid or list of guns with price, damage/fire-rate bars, stock count and a restock timer (OPPS), sometimes a dual price in cash or Robux.
- **HUD while fighting:** ammo by the crosshair or under the hotbar slot, hit markers, a kill feed top left, an "in combat" icon, damage numbers (BlockSpin).
- **Gun collection:** attachments (optics, lasers, drums), skins and camos, cases with posted odds.
- **Safe zones:** spawn and hospitals, sometimes shops, where you can't be shot. Many games show a little shield or "SAFE ZONE" label on the HUD.
- **For Hood Life:** the combat HUD in the redesign spec covers ammo, hit markers and the downed screen. Keep the posted-odds rule for anything random.
