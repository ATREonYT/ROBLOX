# Quiet normal shots make rare shots roar

The one game we measured frame by frame, +1 Stone Skipping, gets its feel from **timing, camera and the counter, not from effects**. A normal throw goes from tap to release in **0.33 s**, the camera follows the stone without a cut, every splash is a plain white crown that is on screen for about **0.15 s**, and a full loop (throw, flight, hold, reward, button back) takes about **6 s**. There is **no camera shake, no FOV punch, no squash and stretch, no hit-stop, no glow and no bloom** on a normal throw ([Frame study](../research_notes/Roblox%20animation%20and%20VFX%20deep%20dive/owner_videos_frame_study.md)). That changes the plan for +1 Cannon Evolution. The normal shot should copy those numbers exactly and stay quiet. All the loud tools the bigger hits use (rarity-coloured teases, hit-stop, camera cutscenes, tinted lighting, server announcements, auras that stay on) then go only to rare shots, and they grow **one layer per tier**, the way Sol's RNG adds new sounds at its 1M+ and 10M+ brackets and saves unique cutscenes and music for the very top ([rblxguide](https://rblxguide.com/games/sols-rng/auras/chromatic-exotic); [allthings.how](https://allthings.how/sols-rng-aura-tier-list-every-rarity-and-drop-rate-ranked/)). Everything a small team needs can be built in code: textures from Python, ring and slash meshes written as OBJ files, cannon motion from springs on Motor6D joints, and one runtime Emit helper that sits inside a phone budget of about **500 draw calls**, where each emitter or beam costs one ([MrChickenRocket, Roblox Staff section](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/real-world-building-and-scripting-optimization-for-roblox/3127146)). The anime games add a ready-made set of moves (impact frames, rock bursts, mesh auras, a meter that fills to an "awakening" state), but several of their habits break our kid-safe rules: strobing impact frames, fake-out summon reveals and luck sold without stated odds. Those are flagged below. One honest limit: apart from our own frame study, **no hit game has published per-beat timings**, and anime move frame data, summon reveal timings and Sol's RNG cutscene seconds were all out of reach. Numbers marked "suggested" are our starting values, to check on a 30 fps capture.

## The reference wins on timing, so the normal cannon shot copies it exactly

The frame study cut 30 fps contact sheets around every key moment in the owner's recordings, and the numbers are consistent across a level-4 tutorial throw and a level-91 throw of 2,552 m ([Frame study](../research_notes/Roblox%20animation%20and%20VFX%20deep%20dive/owner_videos_frame_study.md)). The THROW button rises in **5 frames (0.17 s) with ease-out and no overshoot**, and on tap it drops away in 3 frames. The stone simply appears in the hand with no spawn effect. An **8-frame (0.27 s) wind-up** draws the arm back with ease-in, the release is 1–2 frames with a thin white streak 2–3 frames long, and the camera pans past the thrower toward the water over **0.43 s with ease-in-out and no cut**. There is no hold-to-charge: one tap fires. At the release frame the side HUD slides off in **4 frames**, and the music drops to near silence for about **0.1 s**, a "breath" before the shot. In flight the chase camera is locked to the stone's forward position, so every touchdown lands at the same screen spot, about 60% down. Skips come every **0.53–0.6 s**. Each splash is a white cartoon crown of 3–5 rounded blobs, about 1.5–2× the stone's width, with one flat ripple ring. It peaks on frame 3 and slides out of frame as the camera moves on. A world-anchored "+1", the height of the stone, appears on the splash frame, lives **0.5 s** and fades with no scale pop. The distance counter ticks every frame with no per-tick pop. Short throws ease out over the last 0.8 s, and long throws stop dead.

The ending and landing are just as plain. The counter stops, the shot holds **0.5–0.6 s** on the water while the audio fades, then a **hard cut** goes to a wide shot of the player at home. The win counter already shows the new value on the cut frame, and a loud jingle plays. "+1" with a trophy icon appears instantly above the head, holds 0.8 s and fades in 3 frames. The quest bar drops in from the top in 3–4 frames, a **second cut** to the normal follow camera comes exactly 0.5 s later, 5–8 small trophy icons pop out and float up, and the THROW button returns about 0.9 s after landing. Elsewhere, panels open with the background blur on in 1 frame and a scale curve of 0.10, 0.30, 0.60, 0.85, 1.00, then a **1.08–1.12 overshoot** settling by about 0.35–0.4 s (Back Out). The rebirth white fade is **0.2 s in, 0.4 s held, 0.1 s out**, which is one flash and fits the kid-safe rule, followed by a title pop of 0.5 → 1.05 → 1.0 over 0.33 s with slowly turning rays. Training-pad level-ups fire about 30 flat confetti rectangles in four colours that live 0.6–0.8 s, but the bar itself jumps to each value with no visible tween ([Frame study](../research_notes/Roblox%20animation%20and%20VFX%20deep%20dive/owner_videos_frame_study.md)).

This overrides parts of our earlier cannon recipe. That recipe suggested a 0.25–0.45 s charge, a camera FOV widening from 70 to 80–85, a trauma shake at the shot, a squash-and-stretch ball, hit-stop on landings and a counter pop of 0 → 1.2 → 1.0. None of these show up in the reference on a normal throw, so the normal cannon shot drops them. The table below is the normal shot, measured where the reference gives a number.

| Beat (normal cannon shot) | Time | What it does | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fire button rises | 0.17 s, ease-out, no overshoot | Returns 0.9 s after landing | Measured |
| Tap to ball out | 0.33 s total; charge 0.25–0.3 s | Barrel kicks back and returns on a spring (the cannon's "arm"); no ball squash | Measured timing; cannon motion suggested |
| Audio breath | 0.1 s at the shot | Music ducked to about −20 dB for 3 frames, then the boom | Measured gap; dB suggested |
| Side HUD out / in | 0.13 s out, 0.2 s back | Top bar and Power bar stay | Measured |
| Camera | 2–5 frame hold, then 0.4–0.45 s ease-in-out swing to side-on chase | No shake, no FOV change | Measured; side-on is the owner's Oct 31 call |
| Skips | About 0.55 s apart | White crown, 1.5–2× ball width, peaks frame 3; ripple ring 1× → 3× over 0.35 s | Measured |
| "+1" popup | On the splash frame, 0.5 s life | World-anchored, ball-sized, no pop; Power ticks the same frame with a soft tick | Measured |
| Counter | Every frame | Plain; eases out over 0.8 s on short shots; no end pop (at most 1.1) | Measured |
| End hold, cut home | 0.5–0.6 s hold, hard cut, second cut 0.5 s later | Counters already updated on the cut frame; win jingle | Measured |

Two things are the cannon's own rather than copies. A cannon is a machine, so recoil and a springy return are its version of the thrower's wind-up and follow-through. Keep them short enough to fit the 0.33 s window. And the owner chose a side-on chase, which shows each splash for longer than the reference's behind-the-stone camera does, so the crown should fade out over 0.2–0.3 s rather than relying on the camera to leave it behind ([Frame study](../research_notes/Roblox%20animation%20and%20VFX%20deep%20dive/owner_videos_frame_study.md)).

## Rare shots escalate one layer per tier, and the tease never lies

Every hit with rare outcomes uses the same structure, even though none of them publish seconds per beat. Sol's RNG plays a "star pull" for auras rarer than 1 in 10,000. Its colour and sound step up by rarity bracket, with **distinct sounds for the 1M+ and 10M+ brackets**, so players know how big a roll is before any name appears ([Item Level Gaming](https://itemlevel.net/sols-rng-all-star-pull-animation/); [rblxguide](https://rblxguide.com/games/sols-rng/auras/chromatic-exotic)). Cutscenes grow "progressively more dramatic" with rarity. Only the rarest tier, above about 1 in 1 billion, gets **fully unique cutscenes with custom music**, and auras rarer than about 1 in 100 million notify the whole server ([allthings.how](https://allthings.how/sols-rng-aura-tier-list-every-rarity-and-drop-rate-ranked/)). The top auras then stay on the player: orbiting orbs, stars and a ring-shaped audio visualiser that "pulse in time with the music" ([rblxguide](https://rblxguide.com/games/sols-rng/auras/chromatic-exotic)). Players can turn off cutscenes below a rarity they pick ("Cutscene Skip Rarity"), and can mute the sound other players' rare rolls make ("Server SFX") ([allthings.how guide](https://allthings.how/sol-s-rng-on-roblox-how-to-play-full-guide/); [Item Level Gaming settings](https://itemlevel.net/sols-rng-best-settings-guide/)). Pet Simulator 99 does the social half: "Land something rare and the whole server sees it in global chat", and equipped auras show "your rarest hatch at a glance" to everyone nearby ([BIG Games Update 78](https://www.biggames.io/post/pet-simulator-99-update-78)).

The second shared trick is showing the tier **on the item, all the time**, as an added layer rather than a new model. Evolved Huge pets each get "their own colored trail and light" ([BIG Games Update 36](https://biggames.io/post/pet-simulator-99-update-36)). Murder Mystery 2's Chroma knives keep the base mesh and swap in a "color alternating texture" over a dark base ([MM2 wiki](https://murder-mystery-2.fandom.com/wiki/Chroma_Slasher)). Grow a Garden's Silver, Gold and Rainbow crops are worth 5×, 20× and 50× and look it ([allthings.how](https://allthings.how/grow-a-garden-mutations-all-types-how-to-get-them-stacking/)). For the cannon, this means the barrel keeps a small aura of the player's rarest shot until a better one lands, and a rare ball carries a tier-coloured trail plus a soft light.

Put together with the reference's quiet baseline, this gives the ladder below. All durations and values are **suggested** for our game: no hit game publishes per-tier seconds. Each tier keeps the layers of the tier below and adds **one new kind of layer** (colour and sound, then camera, then freeze and lighting, then sky, then music), and about 0.6–1.5 s. Common and Rare must never interrupt fast firing, so only Legendary and up take the camera. Auto-fire queues big reveals, or plays them as their Rare version if the player turns that on.

| Tier | Total | New layer added | Camera | Text and social |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Common | 0 s | Reference shot exactly | Reference | "+1" only |
| Rare | 0.6 s | Tier-coloured barrel glow from the first frame (pre-welded Neon copy, toggled on), tinted crown, chime | None | "RARE!" in blue, 0.8 s |
| Epic | 1.2 s | Tier trail on the ball, shockwave ring at the muzzle, riser sound | First and only FOV punch (+4 to +8) and one small shake, at the shot only | "EPIC!" with outline; own chat line |
| Legendary | 2.0 s | 0.10 s visual hit-stop at the muzzle, light beam, coin shower; camera-local ColorCorrection | 0.6 s push-in toward the cannon, then back | Name card "LEGENDARY, 1 in N"; gold server line |
| Mythic | 3.0 s | Sky tints toward the accent colour; aura stays on the cannon | Letterbox bars, 90° orbit over 1.5 s | Large card with odds; server banner |
| Secret | 4.5 s, skippable after 1 s | Own 3–4 s music cue, sky swap, ring of stars | Short 3–4 point spline, DepthOfField on the cannon | Full-width card with real stats only; server banner others can mute |

The camera recipe is simple and documented. Set `Workspace.CurrentCamera.CameraType` to `Scriptable`, drive `CFrame`, and update `Focus` every frame, because some visuals pick their level of detail from the distance to the focus ([Creator Docs: Camera](https://create.roblox.com/docs/workspace/camera)). TweenService is fine between two points, but chaining more than two looks poor, which is why CutsceneService uses Bézier curves ([DevForum: CutsceneService](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/718571)). One bug report teaches a rule worth keeping: that module fired `Completed` **before** switching `CameraType` back, which broke a user's script ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/cutsceneservice-smooth-cutscenes-using-b%C3%A9zier-curves/718571/64)). So a `RevealCamera` module should save `CameraType`, `CameraSubject`, `FieldOfView` and `CFrame`, step a Catmull-Rom path on RenderStepped with Sine or Quad InOut, and restore everything in one cleanup that also runs on skip, death and respawn, **before** it fires any "done" signal (suggested). Moon2Cutscene can play Moon Animator 2 camera tracks, but it does not support animation events and all other effects must be scripted by hand ([DevForum: Moon2Cutscene](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/v2-moon2cutscene-play-moon-animator-2-files/3001411?page=3)). Code-driven paths are easier to tune by number. Lighting is the cheapest drama: Bloom, Blur, ColorCorrection, DepthOfField and SunRays parented to the **Camera** show only to that player, while the same effects under Lighting show to everyone ([Creator Docs: Post-processing](https://create.roblox.com/docs/environment/post-processing-effects)). Rare-shot effects belong on the shooter's camera, tweened in over 0.2–0.4 s and out over 0.5–0.8 s, and they must be restored on every exit path, because effects left on are a common bug (suggested).

The kid-safe line is clear. The Xbox photosensitivity guideline fails content at about **3 flashes per second**, or when a flash (a 10% change in brightness) covers about **20% or more of the screen**, and it is stricter for saturated red ([Microsoft XAG 118](https://learn.microsoft.com/de-de/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/118)). A Roblox game was allegedly linked to a player's epilepsy death ([GosuGamers](https://www.gosugamers.net/entertainment/news/78238-roblox-game-allegedly-linked-to-player-s-epilepsy-death-developers-criticised-for-negligence)). Our rule of **one flash per second** sits well inside the guideline, and it should be enforced by one global timestamp gate, not move by move. Sol's RNG's "Breakthrough" layer, a cracked white screen after the star pull ([Item Level Gaming](https://itemlevel.net/sols-rng-all-star-pull-animation/)), becomes a slow fade if we borrow it. Mythic's red theme uses glow and particles, never a red full-screen flash. The tease colour must equal the real tier **from the first frame**. No colour that climbs past the result and drops back, no reel that slows on Mythic and ticks to Common, no "SO CLOSE!" on a Common. Every Rare-and-up card shows "1 in N" with real numbers.

## Our own art comes from Python, OBJ files and three glow tricks

Roblox's own convention makes code-made art practical. Particle textures work best as **PNGs with a transparent background**, the default texture is a white sparkle, and `Color` "tints each particle's texture" ([Creator Docs: Particle emitters](https://create.roblox.com/docs/effects/particle-emitters)). So the house rule is to draw one white or greyscale shape on real alpha and tint it in Roblox. One texture then serves every rarity and every skin accent colour, and the docs say reusing textures costs less memory than unique ones ([same](https://create.roblox.com/docs/effects/particle-emitters)). Greyscale on black also works for additive layers, because at `LightEmission` 1 black adds nothing and disappears. The flip side is that **dark particles at LightEmission 1 vanish**, so smoke and void effects need LightEmission 0 ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/when-coloring-particles-with-lightemission-1-to-black-they-become-transparent/2614320)). Images can be uploaded up to 4096², but mesh texture maps are capped at 1024², and Roblox suggests 256 px for 5-stud objects and 512 px for 10-stud ones ([Creator Docs: Texture specifications](https://create.roblox.com/docs/art/modeling/texture-specifications)). The Asphalt uploader alpha-bleeds images by default "for crisp edges" ([GitHub: asphalt](https://github.com/jackTabsCode/asphalt)). Copying edge colours into the clear pixels is the known fix for dark fringes, so our texture tool should do it too.

The cloud session has Python with Pillow and NumPy but no Blender. Every standard VFX texture can still be made from a formula and committed as a tool (all suggested): a soft glow dot with alpha (1 − r)^2–3; a shockwave ring with alpha exp(−((r − 0.7)/w)²); a crescent slash made the way GIMP artists do it, by drawing streaks of mixed thickness and bending them with a polar-coordinates remap ([DevForum: Crescent slash](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/crecent-sword-slash-effect/1113871)); tiling noise for Beams; gradient strips with a white core line; and 4x4 or 8x8 flipbooks rendered frame by frame with 4–8 px gutters. Posterising alpha to two or three steps gives the flat cel look the anime and cartoony games use, instead of soft photo smoke. Kenney's Particle Pack (80 sprites, CC0) is the one fully safe free pack for reference or a stand-in ([Kenney](https://kenney.nl/assets/particle-pack)). Toolbox "free VFX" usually has no clear licence and can hide scripts, and it would break the house rule that we use only our own art.

Mesh effects follow the same plan. Studio imports **.obj** for single meshes and .fbx/.gltf for anything rigged or vertex-coloured, with a per-object **Make Double Sided** switch that open VFX shells need ([Creator Docs: 3D Importer](https://create.roblox.com/docs/art/modeling/3d-importer.md)). Single meshes must stay under 20,000 triangles ([Creator Docs: Modeling specs](https://create.roblox.com/docs/art/modeling/specifications)). Python can write a 64-segment ring, a 120–200° crescent that tapers with sin(πu), a half-sphere dome or a helix ribbon as OBJ files with U along the sweep and V across it, so a gradient or a scrolling noise reads correctly (suggested). The crescent recipe's key detail is to put the texture on **two `Texture` children** rather than the MeshPart's own TextureID, so the mesh can fade ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/crecent-sword-slash-effect/1113871)). Roblox still has no UV-scroll property. `Texture.OffsetStudsU/V` scrolls on curved meshes but follows surface normals, a Beam laid on the surface scrolls but loses segments on low-end phones, swapping TextureID every frame blows the memory budget, and per-frame EditableMesh UV writes are "the main workable method" for exact flow ([DevForum: Scrollable textures](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/scrollable-textures-for-meshparts/2820890)).

Glow comes from three cheap tricks rather than real lights. Neon with a plain colour plus Bloom gives the core. A **ForceField** shell 5–10% larger gives a moving fresnel rim, which "seems to only really work on spheres or meshes" ([DevForum: ForceField fresnel](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/control-the-fresnel-effect-of-forcefield-material-using-the-reflection-property/258289)). A **Highlight** marks the hero item. The Highlight limit is now **255** on the client, not the 31 that older notes, including our frame study's technique list, still quote. The first Highlight costs up to 1 ms of GPU on mobile and later ones little, but creating or destroying one forces a geometry rebuild, so keep one and change its properties ([Creator Docs: Highlighting](https://create.roblox.com/docs/effects/highlighting)). EditableImage and EditableMesh now work in published games, but only after the owner turns on "Enable Mesh / Image APIs" in the Creator Dashboard. They have strict client memory budgets, EditableImage is capped at 1024² and updates only one image per frame, and EditableMesh is capped at 60k vertices and 20k triangles ([Creator Docs: EditableMesh](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/EditableMesh.md); [EditableImage](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/EditableImage.md)). EditableMesh batching APIs reached published games on Sep 15, 2026 ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/full-release-editablemesh-batching-apis-parallel-queries/4779401)). That makes them a later tool for one hero effect at most, always with a static fallback. Uploading stays with the owner. The Open Cloud Assets API takes one image per call up to 20 MB ([Creator Docs: Assets](https://create.roblox.com/docs/en-us/cloud/guides/usage-assets)), and Asphalt can sync a folder and generate a Luau ID module from the owner's Mac. The no-code path is Studio's Asset Manager bulk import plus a Command Bar helper that prints `[VFXIDS] name=id` lines for the owner to paste back. Unknown IDs stay 0.

## Springs on Motor6D joints give a code-only team pro motion

The tool choice is the first quality decision. TweenService is for fixed one-shot moves that won't be interrupted. A **spring** is for anything that can be retargeted or should overshoot. spr's damping below 1 overshoots, 1 is "the most visually neutral", and its examples are `(1, 1)` for smooth and `(0.6, 4)` for a wobble ([spr](https://www.github.com/Fraktality/spr)). Fusion's springs keep their velocity when the goal changes, so "they handle interruptions smoothly" ([Fusion docs](https://elttob.uk/Fusion/0.3/tutorials/animation/springs/)). A restarted tween snaps its velocity to zero, which is a classic cheap tell. All cosmetic motion runs on the client: a server-tweened platform replicated at roughly 20–30 Hz and stuttered ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/does-tweenservice-optimise-replication/199734)). Measured reference values fit straight onto these tools. A panel pop matching the reference's 1.08–1.12 overshoot is spr at about damping 0.5, frequency 5, on a `UIScale` rather than on Size, so the layout doesn't jump ([Frame study](../research_notes/Roblox%20animation%20and%20VFX%20deep%20dive/owner_videos_frame_study.md)).

The cannon rig is an anchored base plus unanchored, massless parts joined by Motor6Ds: yaw (base to turntable), pitch (turntable to barrel, pivot at the trunnion), recoil (barrel to tube, sliding back on −Z) and wheels (suggested layout). The docs recommend `Motor6D.Transform` "for custom animations rather than C0 and C1", set in `RunService.PreSimulation`, because an Animator overwrites it every frame between PreAnimation and PreSimulation ([Creator Docs: Motor6D](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/Motor6D.md)). One client loop then writes `Recoil.Transform = CFrame.new(0, 0, recoilSpring.x)`. To fire, the spring's position is set back 0.6–1.0 studs instantly, and it returns at damping 0.45, frequency 5 (suggested). A wrong hinge pivot is the most common reason a code rig looks cheap. Aim is plain math (yaw from atan2, pitch from asin of the direction) fed into critically damped springs. `IKControl` with Type LookAt suits a character's head looking at the cannon, and Transform-type IK keeps hands on the handles through recoil ([Creator Docs: IKControlType](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/enums/IKControlType.md)). Bones "only support translation and rotation", so squash on rigid props means changing Size with volume kept (y = s, x = z = 1/√s) ([DevForum: bone scaling request](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/feature-request-bone-scaling-deformation-support/4761420)). Per the frame study, the normal ball doesn't squash at all.

One 2026 change will break old code. Since **Jan 26, 2026**, new R15 player characters spawn with **AnimationConstraint** joints instead of Motor6Ds. `IsA("Motor6D")` misses them, and writing C0, C1, Part0 or Part1 errors. NPCs already in the place and R6 rigs are not upgraded, and props we build keep working on Motor6D ([DevForum: Avatar Joint Upgrade](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/avatar-joint-upgrade-for-physically-simulated-character-movement-is-now-live/4298561)). On upgraded avatars, procedural lean, landing dips and recoil are layered "by multiplying into Transform during PreSimulation", which keeps retargeting intact ([Creator Docs: AnimationConstraint](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/AnimationConstraint.md)). The rule is to multiply onto the Transform the Animator just wrote and never assign a fresh one. Rigs without a Humanoid need an AnimationController with a child Animator to play clips ([Creator Docs: Using animations](https://create.roblox.com/docs/animation/using)). Clips can be built in code (KeyframeSequence, Keyframe and nested Poses) ([Creator Docs: KeyframeSequence](https://create.roblox.com/docs/vi-vn/reference/engine/classes/KeyframeSequence)) and tested through `KeyframeSequenceProvider`, whose IDs are temporary and "cannot be used outside of Studio", so the live game needs an upload ([Creator Docs: KeyframeSequenceProvider](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/KeyframeSequenceProvider.md)). Effects should fire from named markers through `GetMarkerReachedSignal`, so they stay on the contact frame if a clip is re-timed ([robloxapi: AnimationTrack](https://robloxapi.github.io/ref-temp/class/AnimationTrack.html)). Animation Graphs now let a state machine change state on expressions such as `Speed >= 12`, with a default blend of 0.15 s ([Creator Docs: State machines](https://create.roblox.com/docs/animation/graph-editor-state-machine)). Their release status is in conflict across sources, so they should wait until confirmed. For props, springs on Transform beat clips anyway, because they react to fire rate and power and need no upload.

## Budgets and one Emit helper keep effects fast on 2 GB phones

The best hard budget comes from a guest article in the Roblox Staff section, aimed at "most (90%+)" of phones: about **500 draw calls and 500,000 triangles** in the scene, about 150 draw calls for UI, and under **1.3 GB** of client memory for 2 GB phones. Its example splits 300 draw calls for the map and 200 for players, pets, coins and effects. Every ParticleEmitter and every Beam is **one draw call however many particles it emits**, and BillboardGuis "chew up drawcalls very quickly" ([MrChickenRocket](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/real-world-building-and-scripting-optimization-for-roblox/3127146)). Fewer emitters firing more particles beats many small ones. A suggested cap for one big moment is 8–12 emitters and beams, rarely more than about 30 on screen. Since each "+1" BillboardGui costs draw calls too, pool a few and reuse them. The official docs add that particle property changes "can have a dramatic impact on performance", that a 1024² texture uses **four times** the memory of a 512², and that shadows are expensive and switch off entirely below quality level 4 ([Creator Docs: Improve performance](https://create.roblox.com/docs/performance-optimization/improve)). The engine also cuts particle rates by graphics level on its own. One report saw a Rate-30 splash drop from about 15 visible particles to about 1 at minimum quality ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/allow-us-to-disable-vfx-scaling-with-graphics-level/2301568)). So feedback that must survive low quality, like the skip splash, should use `Emit(n)` bursts, whose count defaults to 16 ([Creator Docs: ParticleEmitter](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/ParticleEmitter)), while ambient effects are left to scale.

The runtime system copies one community convention so effects become data the owner can edit in Studio. VFX Forge tags each emitter with **EmitCount, EmitDelay and EmitDuration** attributes ([Forge docs](https://zilibobi.github.io/forge-docs/effects/particle)). Our version keeps templates in `ReplicatedStorage.VFX.<Name>`. One client helper reads those attributes, bursts or enables each emitter on its delay, and cleans up after the longest delay, duration and lifetime. The server only decides the outcome and fires one remote with the effect name, the position and `GetServerTimeNow()`. That pattern is in the official docs: "the server only needs to know the location… while the clients can create visuals locally" ([Creator Docs](https://create.roblox.com/docs/performance-optimization/improve)). `GetServerTimeNow` is accurate to a few milliseconds across clients, by community measurement ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/about-how-accurate-is-getservertimenow/3926695)). Pools should move parts by CFrame, because "CFrame is the only property you can update every frame without heavy code kicking in" ([PartCache](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/partcache-for-all-your-quick-part-creation-needs/246641)). One-off cleanup uses `task.delay` over Debris, by community benchmark ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/hello-ive-been-wondering-if-its-better-to-use-debrisadditem-or-taskdelay/2997135)). The ball needs no physics replication. FastCast2 exists for thousands of projectiles ([FastCast2](https://weenachuangkud.github.io/FastCast2)), but a closed-form arc, p(t) = p0 + v0·t + ½·g·t², computed on each client from the server's start time is simpler for one cannonball, and it also gives the skip points ahead of time (suggested). The same helper is where accessibility lives. Roblox exposes `GuiService.ReducedMotionEnabled`, and its guidance is to replace fly-ins with fades and stop looping motion ([DevForum: Accessibility settings](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/introducing-accessibility-settings/2723187)). Roblox has no flash or shake setting, so our helper adds a toggle, zeroes shake and FOV punches when reduced motion is on, and holds the one-flash-per-second gate.

## Anime games run on a meter, an impact frame and mesh shells

The battlegrounds games share one move structure. In The Strongest Battlegrounds every character has **4 base moves**, and a meter that fills in combat unlocks an Awakening that swaps in **4 ultimate moves**, "sometimes changing normal punches". Kits are built around archetypes: a dash-based fighter, a block-breaking heavy whose "long startup punishes misses", and a cyborg whose "finishers reset cooldowns" ([allthings.how: TSB](https://allthings.how/the-strongest-battlegrounds-character-roster-movesets-and-access-rules/)). Jujutsu Shenanigans' domain ultimate needs a full bar, locks the target out for **7 s** (14 s for the area version, which "freezes every enemy nearby"), then leaves "every skill on cooldown and the Awakening meter at zero" ([Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/roblox-news/jujutsu-shenanigans-0-2-domain-guide)). That built-in cost is the design lesson: a big move feels big because you pay for it. The RPGs earn their biggest form through a long arc. In Type Soul the full release takes raid wins, an NPC that reports progress in **20% steps**, and a final fight against your own weapon spirit that "triggers a cutscene" ([Twinfinite](https://twinfinite.net/guides/all-bankai-progression-type-soul/)). Blox Fruits' Race V4 needs a full moon and a party of different races acting together, and a "thick fog" signals the boss before it spawns ([Gfinity](https://www.gfinityesports.com/roblox/blox-fruits-race-v4/)). Project Slayers describes each element by how it moves: water is "flowing, multi-hit", thunder is near-instant with ground that "breaks and degrades" ([eXputer](https://exputer.com/guides/tier-lists/project-slayers-breathing/)). For a kid cannon game, the meter-to-payoff idea maps onto a rare-shot "overdrive" that fills by firing. The world-cue idea maps onto a sky change a few seconds before a server event. The motion-per-element idea maps onto splash, zap, puff and poke cannon families with no anime names.

The visual vocabulary comes from a few open-source pieces. The impact-frame module is a `ColorCorrectionEffect` with Saturation −1 and **Contrast ±25000 held for 0.1 s**, plus character parts switched to Neon. Its author avoids Highlights because they were "not supported on mobile", and the page's own example uses the wrong key names, so copying it as written falls back to defaults ([DevForum: Impactframe Module](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/open-source-impactframe-module/2963136)). Rock modules raycast the surface normal so rocks face the ground or wall they hit, and pool them with PartCache "to reduce lag" ([DevForum: Rocks Module](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/rocks-module-useful-for-anime-games-skill-effects/1687374/23)). A TSB-inspired version does only the line of ground rocks, with ObjectCache pooling ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/punch-rock-module-inspired-by-tsb/3171422)). Developers asking how to copy TSB's death effect were told to change the victim's body colour and transparency on the server, never the root part ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/how-to-make-the-died-effect-if-a-player-dies-like-the-strongest-battlegrounds/3273149)). The aura, slash and shockwave stacks are the mesh shells from the art section, given a white core, the element colour and a dark edge. Studios mostly buy these effects from freelancers. One lists basic effects at 500–1,000 Robux and full moves at **5,000–10,000 Robux (about $28–52)** ([sst0rmy portfolio](https://sst0rmy.carrd.co)). We build in-house, so the lesson is the method (mesh plus texture plus tween, fired from markers), not the hiring.

Summon banners show how far the genre's monetisation has moved from our rules. Anime Vanguards lists its odds (Rare 75.496%, Epic 20%, Legendary 4%, Mythic 0.5%, **Secret 0.04%**), shows two pity bars (**Legendary guaranteed at 50, Mythic at 400**) and rotates its banner hourly ([DroidGamers](https://www.droidgamers.com/guides/anime-vanguards-banner/)). Anime Last Stand goes down to Ultimate at **0.01%**, sells cheaper pity through VIP (2,400 instead of 3,000 currency), and has a shiny event unit with "no pity" ([ALS Wiki](https://alsroblox.fandom.com/wiki/Summoning_Banners)). Anime Defenders sells a 1,299-Robux Shiny pass and 99/149-Robux luck potions whose effect even a guide admits "we don't know the exact measure" of ([ProGameGuides](https://progameguides.com/roblox/how-to-get-shiny-characters-in-anime-defenders-roblox/)). That breaks Roblox's own rule that luck boosts state their effect in numbers and that the shown odds update while a boost is active ([Creator Docs: Paid random items](https://create.roblox.com/docs/en-us/production/monetization/paid-random-items.md)). What to copy: odds on the card and a visible pity bar. What to leave out: the rest, plus the "fake-out" mid-reveal colour upgrade the genre is known for. No source we could read confirmed that one, and it counts as a near-miss tease under our rules either way.

## Sound carries the hit, so we synthesise four layers and duck the music

The reference's loudest trick is a gap in the music: about 0.1 s of near-silence at the release, then the boom, with a soft tick per skip and the biggest audio peak on the cut home ([Frame study](../research_notes/Roblox%20animation%20and%20VFX%20deep%20dive/owner_videos_frame_study.md)). Blade Ball shows how much sound matters for timing. Guides tell players to turn ball sounds to max because audio reaction (about 140 ms) beats visual reaction (about 250 ms), and its main readable cue is a **white flash ring** when the ball enters parry range ([dungeonpath](https://dungeonpath.com/posts/blade-ball/deflect-parry-timing-guide/); [dungeonpath speed curve](https://dungeonpath.com/posts/blade-ball/speed-curve-guide/)). A good hit is four layers in separate frequency bands: a transient crack mostly above 1 kHz, a mid body near 200 Hz, a sub sine falling through **30–60 Hz over 200–400 ms**, and a dark reverb tail on the body only, with the sub kept dry. Two body layers both near 200 Hz cancel and muddy, and game hits stay "a few hundred milliseconds" ([Violet Recording](https://violetrecording.com/how-to-make-impact-sounds/)). Sound effects sit loud in a game mix: roughly music at −20 dB and hits between −20 and −10 dB, tested on phone speakers ([Krotos](https://krotos.studio/blog/how-to-balance-music-and-sound-effects)). Phones cut the sub, so the crack and a lightly saturated body must carry the shot alone.

All of this fits `tools/sfx/make_sfx.py`, so there are no licensing issues. A normal shot uses the crack and body. Rare tiers add the sub, the tail, a rising riser during the tease and their own sting, and Secret adds its music cue (suggested). On Roblox, SoundGroups are now called "legacy" next to the newer Audio objects but still work. Ducking is a `CompressorSoundEffect` on the Music group with `SideChain` set to the stinger group, and the docs advise "small ducking amounts" ([Creator Docs: Sound groups](https://create.roblox.com/docs/en-us/sound/groups)). Pitch does the rest (suggested): random ±8% on repeated clicks and coins, and one semitone up (×1.0595) per skip, capped at +12, which turns a long flight into a rising scale. The player's own rewards play in 2D at full volume, and other players' rare shots play in 3D with a mute toggle, like Sol's RNG's "Server SFX".

## Discovery now rewards 28-day return, so honest retention is the strategy

The rest of what hits do well matters more since **June 15, 2026**, when Recommended For You moved from a 7-day to a **28-day** view. Signals are now split into Day 1, Days 2–7 and Days 8–28, and Roblox said short-term engagement had let games with eye-catching thumbnails push out games with lasting value ([Roblox Newsroom](https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2026/06/optimizing-discovery-great-games-reach-millions-players-roblox)). The docs list the top signals as play-through rate from impressions, **first-play bounce under 60 s and 61–180 s**, play days per user, and playtime capped at **60 minutes per user per day**, then co-play and spend. A custom thumbnail is required for Home impressions, and it must "accurately reflect your game" ([Creator Docs: Discovery](https://create.roblox.com/docs/discovery.md)). The reference's 6-second loop and instant first reward are exactly what beats the 60-second bounce window. There is also no ranking reason to push sessions past an hour, which is kinder to kids. Since Sep 30, 2025, a game without a maturity label is not playable by the public at all ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/important-updates-unrated-experiences-and-changes-to-experience-pages/3899317)). Rewarded video ads reach only players 13 and older ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/more-creators-can-now-use-rewarded-video-ads/3838678)), so they don't matter for a kid game.

Return and social features are the levers that fit those signals. Grow a Garden's creator credits **weekly updates** and a world that grows while you're away ("You return and your plants have grown bigger"), on the way to a **22.3M peak CCU** ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/?p=305182)). Its weekly scheduled "Admin Abuse" event became news in itself ([Gamepressure](https://www.gamepressure.com/newsroom/what-is-admin-abuse-in-grow-a-garden-gag-we-explain-what-to-expec/z58490)). Its weather events each have their own sky (Night dims with glowing crops, Blood Moon turns the sky red, Black Hole brings a purple void with a portal), each tied to one mutation ([ginx](https://www.ginx.tv/en/weather-events-grow-a-garden)). That is the same "world cue before a big moment" idea the anime RPGs use. For the cannon game, that means a scheduled river event at a published time, a sky tint that tells players luck is boosted, and the real end time shown. Invites use `SocialService:PromptGameInvite` with up to 200 characters of `LaunchData`, which the server reads to place the friend next to the inviter ([Creator Docs: Invite prompts](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/promotion/invite-prompts)). Live leaderboards use MemoryStore sorted maps, whose quota is "1000 + 120 × [concurrent users]" requests per minute ([Creator Docs: Memory stores](https://create.roblox.com/docs/en-us/cloud-services/memory-stores.md)). So one read every 60 s per server, not one per player. Where kids quit gets measured with server-side funnels, up to 10 per game ([Creator Docs: Funnels](https://create.roblox.com/docs/en-us/production/analytics/funnel-events.md)). Saves use ProfileStore, with session locking and auto-save ([DevForum: ProfileStore](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/profilestore/3190543)). On the UI side, the HUD ScreenGui should use `ScreenInsets = CoreUISafeInsets` to stay clear of Roblox's top bar ([Creator Docs: ScreenInsets](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/enums/ScreenInsets.md)). The measured reference rules apply on top: one toast at a time held 3–4 s, cells that grow 5% when pressed, claims that switch in 1 frame.

## Conclusion

The main change this round is in what "polish" means for our game. The earlier assumption was that a pro shot needs shake, FOV punch, squash and hit-stop on every fire. The measured hit uses none of them on a normal throw, and it gets its satisfaction from a 0.33 s release, a camera that never cuts mid-throw, a splash rhythm of about 0.55 s and counters that update on the exact cut frame. That makes the loud toolbox far more valuable, because it becomes the *signal* for rarity rather than background noise. A kid who has seen a thousand quiet shots will notice the first barrel glow, the first FOV punch and the first freeze without reading a word, which is exactly the job Sol's RNG gives its coloured star. The design rule that follows: **the quieter the baseline, the fewer layers each rare tier needs to feel huge**. That suits kid-safety too, since the flash budget is spent only where it means something.

The second takeaway is about where knowledge comes from. Wikis, guides and the DevForum gave the structures (tier ladders, pity bars, impact-frame settings, draw-call budgets), but every *number* that held up came from stepping through 30 fps frames of the owner's own recordings. The cheapest next research step is more of that, not more searching: frame-step one Sol's RNG cutscene compilation, one Pet Simulator 99 Huge hatch and one anime summon reel to replace the suggested tier durations above with measured ones. Then build the Rare tier as the golden slice, one side-by-side picture against the reference's quiet shot, before rolling out the rest of the ladder.
