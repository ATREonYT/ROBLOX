# Escalate Props, Time Effects, Relight the Block

Hood's nine training stations will look progressively cooler only if each tier changes at least two visual channels at once (color, material, silhouette, ornament, effects or pedestal), and if a channel nobody had before arrives every two or three tiers. The loudest jump, continuous motion plus full-body glow plus a halo, belongs to the biggest multiplier jumps: the ×25 Gold Bag and the ×40 Champ Ring. Grow a Garden uses the same pattern: its ×20 Gold mutation only glows, while its ×50 Rainbow mutation cycles color, sheds particles and wears a halo. Professional VFX applies that logic in time. A white-hot flash lands on the impact frame, a tier-colored ring and decelerating sparks follow, and the screen is clear again within about 0.6 seconds. The work is done by the Size, Transparency, Color and Squash curves of a few pooled emitters. Each tier escalates through brightness and one extra layer rather than particle count, which keeps a top-tier punch near 31 particles on a phone. Vibrancy is mostly a lighting problem. Hood's Block sits under a beige Atmosphere at Haze 1.6, with −0.05 saturation and a 17.2 o'clock sun. About thirty lines of lighting code (a cool blue fill, Haze near 0.25, Offset near 0.55, +0.22 saturation, Bloom 0.6 at threshold 1.1) should lift it more than any rebuild. Candy-colored facades, complementary doors and 40–80 Neon parts kept to destinations do the rest. The three topics form one system: the map has to stay a bright, calm stage so that saturated color and glow, reserved for tier props, cash and signs, still mean something. The basics (SmoothPlastic faceted building, sticker UI, anticipation-contact-settle animation, and the retention-first economics that now decide what earns) are in the earlier report, *Craft Hood Like a Toy, Not a Template* (`reports/Cartoony Roblox building UI and animation.md`). This report builds on it and revises two of its recommendations: the daytime lighting preset, and how the UI rarity ladder meets the bag ladder. The egress proxy blocked most game wikis and the DevForum. Engine facts below are checked against Roblox's creator-docs mirror dated October 2, 2026, but descriptions of other games come from search-result summaries, and every Hood-specific number is a starting value to tune on a real phone.

## Tiers read as worth more when several channels climb at once

### Hit simulators escalate color, material, symbols and motion together

No hit simulator relies on a single cue to signal value. Pet Simulator 99 launched with a color ladder of gray Basic, green Rare, blue Epic, orange Legendary, red Mythical, purple Exotic and pink Exclusive. Later updates added Divine yellow, Superior cyan, Celestial pink-and-blue and Secret dark blue. The game also escalates the symbol: one star for Rare, two for Epic, three for Legendary, then four-pointed stars, then octograms ([NamuWiki](https://en.namu.wiki/w/Pet%20Simulator%2099!/%ED%8E%AB); [TV Tropes](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/PetSimulator99)). DevForum builders describe a widely recognized ladder of grey, green, blue, purple, gold and violet, while agreeing that "no single universal standard" exists ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/need-help-on-selecting-value-colors/793985)). Material carries a second ladder. Punch Wall's glove tiers run Bronze, Silver, Gold, Platinum and onward ([progameguides](https://progameguides.com/roblox/punch-wall-codes/)). Gym League escalates one base object by adding material and mythic motifs: Lifting Belt, then Cowboy Belt, Golden Lifting Belt and Championship Belt; Bird Wings, then Angel Wings and Cursed Wings ([Try Hard Guides](https://tryhardguides.com/gym-league-gear-guide-how-to-get-and-rarity/)). Arm Wrestle Simulator saves the diamond material for its rarest arm ([holdtoreset](https://holdtoreset.com/arm-wrestle-simulator-gym/)).

Grow a Garden shows most clearly how the visual jump should track the value jump. A Gold crop is "textured golden with a bright glow" and worth ×20. A Rainbow crop has a 0.1% chance and is worth ×50; it "continuously change[s] colors, emit[s] yellow particles and display[s] a rainbow above". Its lesser mutations each use a simple formula of one tint plus one particle type: dripping water for Wet, blue frost for Chilled, green fog for Zombified ([Grow a Garden Wiki](https://growagarden.miraheze.org/wiki/Mutations); [Theria Games](https://theriagames.com/guide/grow-a-garden-rainbow-mutation/)). Pet Simulator 99 adds an audience. Every pet can hatch as regular, gold, rainbow or shiny, and rare variant hits are announced server-wide ([PS99 Huge Pets](https://pet-simulator.fandom.com/wiki/Huge_Pets_(Pet_Simulator_99)); [PS99 Gargantuan Pets](https://pet-simulator.fandom.com/wiki/Gargantuan_Pets_(Pet_Simulator_99))).

Strength simulators apply the same escalation to places and gates. Muscle Legends unlocks gyms by rebirth count. Frost Gym makes "everything frosted-like". Eternal Gym brings "larger treadmills, new Chests, new Auras". Legends Gym adds "electric effects on the portal" and "the only statue that is glowing white" ([Frost Gym](https://muscle-legends.fandom.com/wiki/Frost_Gym); [Eternal Gym](https://muscle-legends.fandom.com/wiki/Eternal_Gym); [Legends Gym](https://muscle-legends.fandom.com/wiki/Legends_Gym)). Its Muscle King Gym is a single-occupant throne where "only 1 person can train" ([Muscle King Gym](https://muscle-legends.fandom.com/wiki/Muscle_King_Gym)). That makes status visible to everyone, which is what the earlier report found earns return visits. Muscle Legends' bench-press requirements step about ×2–3 (1K, 3K, 7.5K, 15K), close to Hood's ×2–3.3 cost steps from 50 to 50K, so Hood's prices need no change. Strongman Simulator blocks each area's door with a large object that players must be strong enough to drag aside, which makes the gate itself the strength check ([Strongman Simulator](https://roblox.fandom.com/wiki/The_Gang_Stockholm/Strongman_Simulator)). None of these wiki pages could be opened directly, and no source describes per-tier equipment visuals in any boxing game, so the ladder below is a synthesis of these patterns rather than a copy of any one game.

### A nine-station ladder that adds one new channel every two or three tiers

Five rules come out of those games. First, every tier differs from the one below in at least two channels. Second, each rarity break adds a channel no earlier tier had: the first light at the Heavy Bag, the first particles at the Speed Bag, the first moving part at the Double-End Bag, then full-body glow with a halo and floating gems at the Gold Bag, and hue cycling at the Champ Ring. Third, materials climb from junk to prestige: rubber, wood, rope and tape, then canvas and leather, then chrome and steel, then black with metallic trim, then gold, then prismatic Neon. Fourth, the biggest visual jump goes with the biggest multiplier jump. Fifth, the tier color appears in four places at once (trim, nameplate, price label and particle color), so a player can read the ladder from across the courtyard before they can make out any material. `HoodProps.lua`, which is in progress in the repo, already adopts this ladder in its header. `Config/Skins.lua` still gives the Speed Bag orange, the Double-End purple, the Pro Bag teal and the Ring magenta in `S.Stations`, so any sign built from `station.Color` will disagree with the trim. One color per station should drive everything.

| # | Station (cost, mult) | Tier color (RGB) | Body and materials | Frame and pedestal | New channel |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Tire Bag (free, ×2) | Common gray (158,158,158) | Three stacked near-black tires with tread blocks | Rough wood gallows, fabric rope, rusty CorrodedMetal bolts; cracked slab flush with the ground | Gentle swing only |
| 2 | Duct Tape Bag (50, ×3) | Uncommon green (76,217,100) | Faded leather with 3–4 silver Foil tape bands and one dangling strip | Scaffold pipe frame; wooden pallet | Green nameplate |
| 3 | Street Bag (150, ×4) | Rare blue (64,156,255) | Canvas with graffiti stripes, hung on chain | Street-sign or lamp-post frame; painted curb base with blue edge stripe | Faint Neon ground ring |
| 4 | Heavy Bag (500, ×6) | Epic purple (170,85,255) | Glossy red leather (SmoothPlastic, Reflectance about 0.05), white caps, stitched seams | Black steel gallows, four chrome chains to a swivel; DiamondPlate plinth with purple Neon edge | **First PointLight** |
| 5 | Speed Bag (1K, ×8) | Exotic pink (255,90,200) | Leather teardrop | Wood rebound platform on a chrome bracket; one-step plinth | **First particles** (sparkles on hit) |
| 6 | Double-End Bag (3K, ×12) | Superior cyan (60,230,255) | Round leather ball | Floor and ceiling anchors with Neon bungee cords; two-step round plinth | **First moving part** (orbiting ring), electric arcs |
| 7 | Pro Bag (8K, ×18) | Mythic red (255,60,60) | Black leather, red and gold bands, logo plate, gold D-rings | Industrial gallows with red Neon; stage with corner posts | Embers, spotlight cone, rotating floor emblem |
| 8 | Gold Bag (20K, ×25) | Legendary gold (255,200,60) | Full gold body | Gold and white marble gallows; round gold dais with steps | **Full glow, rotating halo, bobbing gems, god-ray** |
| 9 | Champ Ring (50K, ×40) | Celestial hue cycle | Canvas with a spinning belt hologram above | Four posts, hue-cycling Neon ropes, turnbuckle pads; raised 3-stud platform with apron, steps and spotlights | **Hue cycling**, confetti on unlock |

The pedestal is the quietest tier signal and the easiest to build from code. Its height and step count climb from flush slab to pallet, curb, plinth, one step, two steps, stage, gold dais and ring, and the nameplate can carry one to three stars or chevrons the way Pet Simulator 99 does.

The ladder collides with the earlier report's UI rarity ladder in one place. There, Legendary gold (#FFB020) ranks below Mythic red (#FF3B5C); here, the red Pro Bag sits below the Gold Bag. The Gold Bag's identity is its material, not a flat trim color, so the cleanest fix is to share the earlier tokens for the gray, green, blue, purple and red tiers, so that a Rare bag and a Rare Crew card are the same blue. The Gold Bag and the Champ Ring would then appear in the UI as material tiers above the color ladder, with a gold gradient and shine sweep and a rainbow stroke, not as Legendary cards. The alternative is to swap the top two UI colors before any Crew rarity ships.

Gold needs special handling because Roblox metal reads as metal only when it has something to reflect. `Lighting.EnvironmentSpecularScale` defaults to 0, and the docs say raising it "is especially important to make metal look more realistic" ([Lighting.yaml](https://github.com/Roblox/creator-docs/blob/main/content/en-us/reference/engine/classes/Lighting.yaml)). Community threads add that the Metal material ignores `Reflectance`, so shiny gold is usually SmoothPlastic with Reflectance, environment scales near 1 and a detailed skybox ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/is-it-possible-to-make-a-part-shiny-gold/1715930)). That conflicts with the vibrancy preset later in this report, which lowers specular scale to 0.25 so the street's 49 Metal parts stop mirroring a gray sky. Because the setting is global, the Gold Bag should earn its shine locally. Use SmoothPlastic gold with Reflectance of 0.1–0.25, Foil accents, star glints, and a periodic specular sweep: a thin white Neon bar, 0.15 studs wide, tweened diagonally across the bag's front from Transparency 0.2 to 1 every 2.5–4 seconds. Add a warm PointLight. `SurfaceAppearance` emissive masks are the other route to glowing logos and price text. The docs describe them as "more artistic control than the all-or-nothing Neon material" ([surface-appearance.md](https://github.com/Roblox/creator-docs/blob/main/content/en-us/art/modeling/surface-appearance.md)).

### Locked stations should form a visible staircase, with only the next one teasing

Hits gate content with physical, in-world objects: Muscle Legends' rebirth portals, Strongman's blocked doors and Pet Simulator 99's server-wide brags. Hood already turns locked bags into black silhouettes (`hood/README.md`). We propose turning that into a four-state staircase. **Far-locked tiers**, two or more steps ahead, stay dark: a Highlight with a near-black fill (FillTransparency 0.1–0.2), the tier color as the outline and `DepthMode` Occluded, plus a "???" label with the price in a BillboardGui (MaxDistance 60–80, LightInfluence 0). Their pedestals and tier trims stay lit, so the whole color ladder is visible from spawn. **The next tier** is the only teaser. It shows in full color, either behind a translucent ForceField or Glass box or wrapped in chains with a padlock, under a pulsing tier-colored beam, with a live progress bar and a ghosted "x4". **An affordable tier** shakes its padlock and turns its label green. **The unlock** drops the chains (unanchor plus tween), fires a burst in the tier color with a sound and a camera pulse, swings the bag in from above and shows a "NEW BAG: Street Bag x4" toast; Gold Bag and Ring unlocks are announced server-wide.

The engine details matter. Highlights now allow **255 simultaneous instances per client**, and a disabled one still takes a slot ([Highlight.yaml](https://github.com/Roblox/creator-docs/blob/main/content/en-us/reference/engine/classes/Highlight.yaml)). So the silhouette Highlight should be retuned in place when a bag unlocks, not destroyed. Price and multiplier labels belong in a BillboardGui about 1.5–2 studs above the bag top, so they read from any angle. The permanent nameplate belongs in a SurfaceGui on the pedestal front. For SurfaceGui text, the docs say "higher values will cause the various GuiObjects within to appear smaller… lower values will cause objects to appear larger" ([SurfaceGui.yaml](https://github.com/Roblox/creator-docs/blob/main/content/en-us/reference/engine/classes/SurfaceGui.yaml)). A simulator builder fixed tiny "Cash Needed" text by lowering `PixelsPerStud` ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/large-surfacegui-text-for-simulator-display/3083555)). About 50 pixels per stud with LightInfluence 0 keeps nameplates readable at a distance and lit at night. No source measures which locked-state style converts best, so this is a first design to test.

## Props read as real through oversized hardware, not polygon counts

### Real bags are defined by D-rings, chains, swivels and caps

Equipment makers describe the hardware that makes a prop read as a real bag. A heavy bag "typically ha[s] four top D-rings", and "the swivel then connects to the center of the chain assembly" ([Revgear](https://revgear.com/products/heavy-duty-chain-and-swivel); [Title Boxing](https://www.titleboxing.com/punching-bags/heavy-bag-accessories)). A speed bag hangs under "a finished wood platform" on a heavy-duty steel frame ([Title Boxing](https://www.titleboxing.com/punching-bags/heavy-bag-accessories)). A double-end bag is anchored to floor and ceiling with "bungees and carabiners" ([Meister](https://meisterelite.com/products/meister-double-end-speed-bag-d-ring-anchor-mounting-kit-w-bungees)). Four ordinary 23-inch tires weigh about 80–90 lb and work as an uppercut or heavy bag ([6 Dragons Kung Fu](https://www.6dragonskungfu.com/how-to-make-a-punching-bag-with-tires/)). A professional four-rope ring has **16 turnbuckles**, ropes at about 40, 70, 100 and 130 cm, corner pads in red, blue and two whites, and an apron extending about 85 cm past the ropes ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boxing_ring); [Deportrainer](https://www.deportrainer.com/en/blog-deportrainer/78_boxing-ring-parts)).

The cartoon version keeps every real part, blows the hardware up to 2–3× real proportions and sizes everything against the 5-stud avatar from the earlier report. The proposed dimensions below are design inference. They make the ring larger than a real one so that several players fit inside.

| Prop | Proposed stud dimensions |
|---|---|
| Heavy bag | 2.4–3 diameter, 5–6 tall, bottom about 1.5 above the floor, so fists at about 3 studs hit the middle third |
| Speed bag | 1.2 × 1.6 teardrop under a 4 × 3 platform at about 5.5 |
| Double-end ball | 1.6 diameter at chest height, bungees to a floor anchor and an overhead beam |
| Tire bag | 3–4 tires, each 3.2 diameter × 1.2 wide |
| Champ Ring | 24–28 inside the ropes; platform 2.5–3 high; apron 2; posts 6–7; ropes at 1.5, 2.6, 3.7 and 4.8 above the canvas, 0.35–0.45 thick |

Each bag then gets a detail kit of one to four parts per item: end caps 1.05× the body diameter in a contrasting color, two or three darker horizontal seam bands standing slightly proud of the body, four vertical seam lines, a logo plate, four 0.5-stud D-rings with a four-chain "spider" up to one swivel, and a strap with a buckle at mid-height. Low tiers add worn patches, tape strips, a dangling tape end and stuffing poking out. High tiers swap these for clean leather and gold hardware. The silhouette should bulge 5–10% wider at the middle than at the caps. Anything round in real life stays visibly round (no four-sided "cylinders"). A few thick shapes beat many thin ones. Every station uses a body color, a cap or trim color and a metal color, plus the tier color as its accent. The ring kit is a dark apron skirt with logo SurfaceGuis on all four faces, corner posts padded red, blue, white and white, three turnbuckle pads per corner, one or two vertical rope spacers per side, steps at a neutral corner, a center logo on the canvas and four overhead spotlights. No source documents how existing Roblox boxing games model their bags or rings, so these kits are original design.

### Parts carry most of the work, and five tricks remove the blockiness

A parts-only bag is cheap, fully authored in Luau and easy to recolor per tier. The five Part shapes (Ball, Block, Cylinder, Wedge and CornerWedge) ([Part.yaml](https://github.com/Roblox/creator-docs/blob/main/content/en-us/reference/engine/classes/Part.yaml)) become much less blocky with five tricks. A Sphere `SpecialMesh` "can be freely resized on all axis", which gives **ellipsoids** and flattened caps; it changes only appearance, so Material doesn't render on it and collision stays with the parent part ([SpecialMesh.yaml](https://github.com/Roblox/creator-docs/blob/main/content/en-us/reference/engine/classes/SpecialMesh.yaml)). Eight edge-rotated blocks make an **octagon** ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/making-a-octogon/1120802)), which in code generalizes to N blocks rotated about Y, each `2·r·tan(π/N)` wide, for faceted plinths and ring platforms. A **rounded block** combines a core block, a thinner shell plate on each face and a cylinder along each edge, sunk until only a quarter sticks out ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/how-to-make-a-rounded-block/2387408)). A **cylinder rim** comes from duplicating the cylinder twice, negating both copies and unioning them with the original ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/ways-to-create-outline-on-a-cylinder/1116612)). Wedges and corner wedges along the edges give **bevels** ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/how-to-make-corners-bevels-and-curves-specifically-from-classic-builds/3523451)).

With these, a bag body becomes three stacked cylinders with the middle one 1.06× wider, capped by flattened spheres. A tire becomes a dark cylinder with a slightly smaller, darker sidewall disc on each face and 16–24 tread blocks spaced 2π/N around it. Long chain runs become a Beam with a chain texture, or a visible `RopeConstraint`, rather than dozens of links. Practitioners say Roblox cylinders extend along their local X axis. That is unverified here, so check it once in Studio before generating rotations.

The budget question is draw calls, not parts. Community testing finds that two parts cost two draw calls and their union costs one, but unions "can glitch and even create more triangles". Meshes win for repeated objects because one mesh ID is reused ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/performance-question-parts-or-unions/1360129); [DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/why-meshes-are-superior-to-unions-and-how-to-easily-convert-them/625585)). `GeometryService:UnionAsync` performs CSG from a script with `CollisionFidelity`, `RenderFidelity` and `SplitApart` options. CSG needs watertight input. `SweepPartAsync`, which drags a part along a CFrame path into a MeshPart, is still in beta ([solid-modeling.md](https://github.com/Roblox/creator-docs/blob/main/content/en-us/parts/solid-modeling.md)).

We propose rough part budgets per station:

| Stations | Parts |
|---|---|
| Tiers 1–3 | 25–50 |
| Tiers 4–7 | 50–90 |
| Gold Bag | about 100 |
| Champ Ring | 150–250 |

Union static decorative clusters once at build time, but keep tier-colored trim as separate parts so code can recolor it. Most importantly, build the repeated details (D-ring, swivel, turnbuckle pad, logo plate) from one Luau builder function. All nine stations then share the same quality and differ only in palette, material and effects.

### Generate meshes once in Studio, then reuse one mesh ID across all nine stations

Meshes earn their place for round hero shapes that repeat across tiers: the bulging bag body with seams, the speed-bag teardrop, the tire torus, the glove, D-rings and turnbuckle pads. A code-first team doesn't need Blender for these. Roblox's `EditableMesh` builds geometry from script with `AssetService:CreateEditableMesh()`. Running it inside a published game is a poor fit, though. It "fails by default", requires the owner to be **13+ age-verified and ID-verified** with **Enable Mesh / Image APIs** switched on, and runs under strict client memory budgets. Studio and plugins have no such limits ([EditableMesh.yaml](https://github.com/Roblox/creator-docs/blob/main/content/en-us/reference/engine/classes/EditableMesh.yaml)).

The practical pipeline therefore runs the mesh code once, in Studio. A Luau command-bar script or local plugin builds each prop as an EditableMesh, and `AssetService:CreateAssetAsync(em, Enum.AssetType.Mesh, …)` uploads it; this call "can only be used in locally loaded plugins and uploads assets without prompting first" ([AssetService.yaml](https://github.com/Roblox/creator-docs/blob/main/content/en-us/reference/engine/classes/AssetService.yaml)). The returned IDs go into a `MeshIds` ModuleScript under Rojo, and at runtime `AssetService:CreateMeshPartAsync(Content.fromAssetId(id), {CollisionFidelity = …})` creates the props, since `MeshPart.MeshId` is read-only. One gap remains: the docs tie the verification toggle only to EditableMesh, but nobody here has confirmed that `CreateMeshPartAsync` on an ordinary uploaded mesh skips it. Test that before committing.

The fallback is to write `.gltf` or `.glb` files with Lune or Python and import them. Open Cloud's Model upload accepts `.fbx`, `.gltf`, `.glb`, `.rbxm` and `.rbxmx`, but not `.obj` ([usage-assets.md](https://github.com/Roblox/creator-docs/blob/main/content/en-us/cloud/guides/usage-assets.md)). Only `.fbx` and `.gltf` carry hierarchies, PBR textures and vertex colors ([importer.md](https://github.com/Roblox/creator-docs/blob/main/content/en-us/studio/importer.md)). glTF also avoids the FBX scale trap: Blender's default FBX export imports "at an unexpectedly large scale", while glTF needs no scale settings ([blender.md](https://github.com/Roblox/creator-docs/blob/main/content/en-us/art/blender.md)).

Generated meshes fail in predictable ways, and the specs name them. A mesh "can not exceed 20,000 triangles" (a stylized bag needs only 300–1,500), and it must be watertight, free of N-gons and never zero-thickness ([specifications.md](https://github.com/Roblox/creator-docs/blob/main/content/en-us/art/modeling/specifications.md)). A face is visible only when "the vertices go counterclockwise around it". Normals live per face corner, so shared vertices shade smooth and hard cartoon edges need separate normals per face, and new vertices default to UV (0,0) and white ([EditableMesh.yaml](https://github.com/Roblox/creator-docs/blob/main/content/en-us/reference/engine/classes/EditableMesh.yaml)). Author at one unit per stud with Y up, and put each prop's origin where it hangs or sits, because the Importer sets the pivot to the scene origin by default. A mesh takes **one material** ([texture-specifications.md](https://github.com/Roblox/creator-docs/blob/main/content/en-us/art/modeling/texture-specifications.md)), so trims, chains and caps should be separate parts, which also keeps them recolorable. Collision should be Box or Hull with an invisible hit box for punches, and the Automatic `RenderFidelity` drops detail beyond 250 and 500 studs ([meshes.md](https://github.com/Roblox/creator-docs/blob/main/content/en-us/parts/meshes.md)).

For the cartoon look, community low-poly guides favor flat shading, small bevels and few or no textures ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/introduction-to-low-poly-and-blender-how-to-create-an-island/424785)). Roblox officially recommends vertex painting for stylized work, because it colors meshes "without the need for UV mapping or image textures" and "can reduce texture memory usage and draw calls" ([blender.md](https://github.com/Roblox/creator-docs/blob/main/content/en-us/art/blender.md)).

Per-tier recoloring of a single textured mesh is the most useful trick. `SurfaceAppearance.Color` tints as a multiplier, so "authoring your original ColorMap in near-white grayscale colors creates the strongest tinting effect". Tinting "does not affect performance". `AlphaMode = TintMask` tints only the areas the alpha marks, which means only the trim can take the tier color ([surface-appearance.md](https://github.com/Roblox/creator-docs/blob/main/content/en-us/art/modeling/surface-appearance.md)). Texture size should follow the object: the spec suggests 256² for an object about 5 studs across and 512² for 10 studs ([texture-specifications.md](https://github.com/Roblox/creator-docs/blob/main/content/en-us/art/modeling/texture-specifications.md)).

## Professional VFX is timing, value and layering, not particle count

### Pros let gameplay set loudness and value set focus

Professional real-time VFX artists work from a short list of principles that transfer directly to Roblox. Riot's League of Legends VFX style guide rests on five pillars: gameplay, value, color, shape and timing. Its rule is that "visual impact should represent gameplay impact": frequent small effects stay small, and rare big ones get loud ([Riot](https://nexus.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/2017/10/dev-leagues-vfx-style-guide/)). VFX Apprentice calls value ranges "the most powerful tools… to dictate where a player's eyes focus". It says saturation, value contrast and size "must align directly with a spell's mechanical tier" ([VFX Apprentice](https://www.vfxapprentice.com/blog/10-league-of-legends-vfx-design-tips)). On timing, 80.lv's summary is that "80% of impact is before contact". The climax should carry "the maximum number of elements with biggest contrast and high saturation", and dissipation "shouldn't stay visible for long" ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/vfx-staples-shape-color-and-motion)). These three sources were read only through search summaries.

Roblox's own docs agree. Fading particles in and out "avoids a popping in/out effect" ([Particle emitters](https://create.roblox.com/docs/effects/particle-emitters)). A moving effect in a still world draws players' eyes ([Core curriculum](https://create.roblox.com/docs/tutorials/curriculums/core/building/create-basic-visual-effects)). For Duvall Drive's lightning, Roblox's team randomized texture, brightness and delay on every strike ([Duvall Drive](https://create.roblox.com/docs/resources/the-mystery-of-duvall-drive/develop-a-moving-world)).

For Hood, those principles become a timing template for every punch, matching the hit-stop and camera kick already in `Juice.lua`.

| Phase | Duration | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Wind-up | 80–150 ms | Glove trail switches on; faint charge particles from tier 5 up |
| Impact frame | 0–60 ms | White-hot core flash, ring and sparks at full contrast; Highlight fill flash; 40–80 ms hit-stop |
| Dissipation | 150–450 ms | Sparks slow under drag, ring fades, dust drifts |
| Clean slate | by 0.6 s | Nothing left, so rapid punching never stacks clutter |

Every glow follows a **white-hot to tier color** ramp: white at birth, tier color by about a third of its life, a darker tier color at death. Cores stay near-white and the hue lives on the edges and secondary elements, which keeps tiers distinguishable on a small screen.

Effects layer into three ranks: **primary** elements (core flash and ring) are the brightest and largest, **secondary** elements (sparks and motes) are smaller and more saturated, and **tertiary** elements (dust and smoke) are low-contrast and non-additive. Shape follows theme: round puffs and stars for friendly moments, shards and streaks for power and electricity, rays and glints for gold. Every shape stays at least 0.3 studs at bag distance, because one-pixel sparks vanish on phones. Lifetime, Speed, Rotation and RotSpeed always take min–max ranges, and Size takes a 10–20% envelope.

### Shape four curves, then choose blend, motion and spawn point

A ParticleEmitter draws textured quads. Most of its look comes from four curves over each particle's lifetime: Size, Transparency, Color and Squash. Three choices do the rest.

**Spawn point.** Parented to a part, particles fill its volume. Parented to an Attachment, they start from a point, though the Sphere and Cylinder shapes "will not display correctly" there ([Particle emitters](https://create.roblox.com/docs/effects/particle-emitters)).

**Blend.** `LightEmission = 1` is additive and lights nothing in the world. Darker colors on an additive emitter read as more transparent. `LightInfluence` decides how much scene lighting darkens particles. Its default depends on how the emitter was created: "1 if inserted with Studio tools. If inserted using `Instance.new()`, it is 0" ([ParticleEmitter API](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/ParticleEmitter)). Hood builds every effect in code, so its emitters start full-bright. That suits glows, but dust and smoke need about 0.6 set explicitly. `Brightness` scales emitted light when LightInfluence is 0. Raising it to 1.5–3 on top tiers pushes their particles over the Bloom threshold.

**Motion.** `Drag` is the half-life of a particle's speed, so a high `Speed` with Drag 8–12 gives the snappy "explode then hang" feel that `Juice.burst` already uses. `Orientation = VelocityParallel` with Squash of at least 1 makes streaks point where they fly. `VelocityPerpendicular` makes flat ground rings. `FacingCameraWorldUp` suits flames and steam that shouldn't roll with the camera. `ZOffset` pushes glints in front of the bag (+0.5 to +2) or dust behind players (negative). `TimeScale = 0` freezes particles for hit-stop.

The engine sets some limits. Lifetime caps at 20 seconds, and emission at "400 particles per second (100 per second on mobile)" per emitter ([Particle emitters](https://create.roblox.com/docs/effects/particle-emitters)). Roblox's own dust-motes tutorial sets Rate to 50,000, though, so how strictly the cap is enforced is unclear ([Core curriculum](https://create.roblox.com/docs/tutorials/curriculums/core/building/create-basic-visual-effects)). `VelocitySpread` is deprecated in favor of `SpreadAngle`. `FastForward()` exists but game scripts can't call it ([Roblox-Client-Tracker API dump](https://github.com/MaximumADHD/Roblox-Client-Tracker)).

Flipbooks come in 2×2, 4×4, 8×8 or custom grids, with a 30 fps maximum. OneShot mode spans the particle's life, which suits puffs. Random mode crossfades frames for twinkling. `FlipbookStartRandom` with a framerate of 0 gives each particle a random static frame, so a single white-tinted emitter can throw multi-colored confetti or varied shards. Clients "automatically deactivate flipbooks when they are low on memory" ([Particle emitters](https://create.roblox.com/docs/effects/particle-emitters)). The sheet-size rule is contested. The API says the texture must be an exact multiple of the layout, the engine's error string still demands 1024×1024, and search summaries report the rule was loosened to any power-of-two square from 8 to 1024 ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/particles%E2%80%99-flipbook-release/2029388)). Power-of-two squares satisfy all three readings.

Bursts use a disabled emitter and `:Emit(n)`. Roblox's explosion recipe emits 100 ([Explosions tutorial](https://create.roblox.com/docs/tutorials/use-case-tutorials/vfx/use-particles-for-explosions)). `Emit()` doesn't yield, and destroying the emitter's parent right after it removes the particles before they render ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/issues-with-particleemitteremit/3325717)). Roblox publishes exact values for several base recipes, and Hood's effects can start from them.

### Beams, trails, Highlights and gradients glow without particle cost

Beams are textured ribbons that follow a cubic Bézier between two Attachments. `CurveSize0` pulls the first control point along Attachment0's +X axis, and `CurveSize1` pulls the second along Attachment1's −X axis ([Beams](https://create.roblox.com/docs/effects/beams)). A scrolling `TextureSpeed` turns a beam into an energy stream, rope glow or light shaft. A beam with no texture renders as a solid colored line, and a Color or Transparency sequence with n keypoints needs at least n−1 `Segments` ([Beam API](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/Beam)).

Roblox built Duvall Drive's hero lightning from scripted textured beams. The server picked random parameters and fired them to all clients, which set the texture and Brightness 10, then tweened brightness, Bloom and color correction down in sync with the audio. The bolt textures were a squiggly line with an "Outer Glow" layer effect ([Duvall Drive](https://create.roblox.com/docs/resources/the-mystery-of-duvall-drive/develop-a-moving-world)). Hood can get lightning before any upload. Chain four to six untextured beams with `Segments = 1` through jittered attachments, and re-randomize the joints three times over about 0.15 seconds for the Double-End Bag's arcs. The open-source `Lightning-Beams` module does the same with Perlin noise ([GitHub](https://github.com/SamyBlue/Lightning-Beams)). A flat aura ring around a pedestal takes four beams through four attachments at 90°, with CurveSize about 0.55× the radius, `TextureMode = Wrap` and a TextureSpeed that rises with tier.

Trails record two attachments' paths. Shortening `Lifetime` "moves through the space more quickly… a faster, more animated visual appearance" ([Trails](https://create.roblox.com/docs/effects/trails)). For fist trails, put two attachments about 0.35 studs apart on each glove. Use a Lifetime of 0.12 seconds at tier 1, rising to 0.2 at tier 8, with WidthScale tapering from 1 to 0 and FaceCamera on. Keep LightEmission at 0 for the low tiers, where a solid white streak reads in daylight, and raise it to 0.6–1 from tier 5. Enable the trail at the end of the wind-up and disable it shortly after impact.

Highlights draw an outline plus a fill, and their cost profile shapes how Hood should use them. Toggling `Enabled` "has no performance impact" and property changes are lightweight. **Adding or removing a Highlight can trigger a geometry rebuild**, causing spikes and extra draw calls. The first visible Highlight costs "up to 1 millisecond of GPU time on mobile", and invisible ones cost nothing ([Highlighting objects](https://create.roblox.com/docs/effects/highlighting)). `Juice.flash` today creates and destroys a Highlight on every punch, which is exactly the churn the docs warn about. A persistent Highlight per bag, tweened between fully transparent and flashed, fixes it:

```lua
-- One Highlight per bag, created once; punches only tween its fill.
local TweenService = game:GetService("TweenService")
local FLASH = TweenInfo.new(0.12, Enum.EasingStyle.Quad, Enum.EasingDirection.Out)
local function bagHighlight(model: Model, outline: Color3?, outlineT: number?): Highlight
	local h = model:FindFirstChild("TierHighlight") :: Highlight?
	if not h then
		h = Instance.new("Highlight")
		h.Name, h.DepthMode = "TierHighlight", Enum.HighlightDepthMode.Occluded
		h.FillColor, h.FillTransparency = Color3.new(1, 1, 1), 1
		h.Parent = model
	end
	h.OutlineColor, h.OutlineTransparency = outline or Color3.new(1, 1, 1), outlineT or 1
	return h
end
local function hitFlash(h: Highlight)
	h.FillTransparency = 0.25
	TweenService:Create(h, FLASH, { FillTransparency = 1 }):Play()
end
```

Neon glows only through Bloom. Its glow also needs higher graphics settings: search summaries report roughly quality level 8 or above, and that "Automatic" may not bloom at all ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/neon-part-not-glowing/3150111)). Particles, beams and trails light nothing ([ParticleEmitter API](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/ParticleEmitter)). Every Neon part should therefore be a bright, solid color paired with a shadowless PointLight or SurfaceLight, so it still reads on a phone with Bloom off.

GUI objects have no blend-mode property anywhere in the API, so additive GUI glow is impossible natively ([API dump](https://github.com/MaximumADHD/Roblox-Client-Tracker)). `UIGradient` now offers Radial and Conical types with Repeat and Mirror tiling ([Appearance modifiers](https://create.roblox.com/docs/ui/appearance-modifiers)). That gives Hood textureless halos: a BillboardGui with LightInfluence 0 and Brightness 2–3, holding a round Frame with a Radial gradient from white to the tier color. A Conical gradient with Repeat at a Scale of about 0.125 should produce eight sun-rays behind the Gold Bag. That combination is untested.

Mesh effects fill the gaps. A Ball part with ForceField or Neon material, tweened up in size and toward full transparency with Quart Out easing, makes a shockwave. A flat Neon cylinder makes a ground disc. ForceField material animates itself, with the texture's red channel driving which pixels show ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/how-forcefield-texture-animation-works/3041374)).

The legacy `Explosion` object is not deprecated, but it is the wrong tool for wall breaks. By default it breaks joints, kills unprotected Humanoids and craters terrain. If one is ever used, the docs advise setting `DestroyJointRadiusPercent = 0` ([Explosion API](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/Explosion)).

### Budget fill-rate, scale bursts yourself, and keep every effect on the client

The GPU cost of effects is mostly screen coverage and overdraw, not raw particle count. "The more pixels particles occupy on a player's screen, the more costly it is on the GPU", and "the more layers of transparent effects on screen, the more costly" ([Particle emitters](https://create.roblox.com/docs/effects/particle-emitters)). Particles don't batch well, and "property changes to ParticleEmitters can have a dramatic impact on performance" ([Improve performance](https://create.roblox.com/docs/performance-optimization/improve)). So Hood should toggle emitters rather than tween their properties, and animate parts, lights, beams and GUIs instead. Big soft layers like flashes, smoke and halos should live under 0.3 seconds.

Community figures put practical budgets at 150–300 live particles on mobile and 500–800 on desktop. Those come from a third-party blog and are heuristics ([kitsblox](https://kitsblox.com/blog/roblox-particle-system-deep-dive)). Graphics quality reportedly scales `Rate` but not `Emit()` ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/graphics-quality-lowering-particle-emission-rates/1241979)). A recent Engine Bugs thread is titled "Particle Emitters no longer change Rate with graphics quality", and it couldn't be read ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/particle-emitters-no-longer-change-rate-with-graphics-quality/4834736)). Hood should therefore look acceptable whether or not the engine throttles, and scale bursts itself. Game scripts can read `UserGameSettings.SavedQualityLevel`, though not the true automatic level ([API dump](https://github.com/MaximumADHD/Roblox-Client-Tracker)).

```lua
local UIS = game:GetService("UserInputService")
local UserGameSettings = UserSettings():GetService("UserGameSettings")
local function vfxScale(): number
	local q = UserGameSettings.SavedQualityLevel
	local s = if q == Enum.SavedQualitySetting.Automatic then 1 else math.clamp(q.Value / 10, 0.35, 1)
	if UIS.TouchEnabled and not UIS.KeyboardEnabled then s *= 0.65 end -- phones and tablets
	return s
end
local function emitScaled(pe: ParticleEmitter, n: number)
	pe:Emit(math.max(1, math.round(n * vfxScale())))
end
```

We propose the budget sheet below. Ambient particles stay at four or fewer per visible bag for tiers 1–3, 12 or fewer for tiers 4–6 and 18 or fewer for tiers 7–8, which comes to about 80 with eight bags on screen. Map ambience gets about 100 more. Bursts stay at or under 31 per punch, so rapid punching keeps 60–80 burst particles alive. A rebirth may spend about 150 for a second or two.

The other budgets are short. One persistent Highlight per bag from tier 6 up, plus the local character, and none created per hit. About one shadowless light per bag from tier 4 up, plus the ring's four SpotLights. Beams use one segment when straight and 8–12 when curved. No more than four unique flipbook sheets, tinted per tier with `Color`. A client loop every half second switches each bag's ambient emitters and lights off beyond about 70 studs.

The architecture follows Roblox's Duvall Drive pattern. The server sends `{kind, tier, pos, dir}` over Hood's existing `Net` remotes, and clients build and render everything. Ambient bag effects are built on each client from a `Tier` attribute, so the server never replicates an emitter. Before shipping, test at Studio quality levels 1 and 21 and on a low-end Android phone set to Automatic. Every tier must stay distinguishable without Bloom, and additive effects must stay readable against the daytime sky that the next section makes brighter.

## Vibrancy comes from light and grading before geometry

### The 2025–26 front page is a bright, calm stage with loud collectibles

The current charts are led by games whose maps are deliberately simple. Steal an Egg, released July 25, 2026, was the most-played Roblox game in September 2026 at **2,147,141 concurrent players** ([rblxdb](https://rblxdb.com/best-roblox-games)). Grow a Garden 2 launched June 12, 2026. It replaced the original's row-based plots with "a circular map where player gardens surround a central hub containing shops" and added a day/night cycle ([games.gg](https://games.gg/news/grow-a-garden-2-release-date/)). Steal a Brainrot runs "a fairly simple map that undergoes various changes depending on the active event": a conveyor down the middle with bases on both sides ([Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/roblox-news/steal-brainrot-map-guide)).

Color in these games carries information. Brainrot bases are gated by red laser beams, finishing a mutation index earns a base in that mutation's color such as golden or Rainbow, and Lucky Blocks are color-coded by rarity ([Steal a Brainrot fandom](https://stealabrainrot.fandom.com/wiki/Base)). Steal an Egg strings ten biomes along a narrow map, each gated by a speed requirement, so progress reads as a sequence of color-themed zones ([games.gg](https://games.gg/roblox/guides/steal-an-egg-biomes-guide/)). In Plants vs Brainrots, "the sunny sky is the default state of the game world", which the game returns to after every weather event ([Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/roblox-news/plants-vs-brainrots-weather-guide)). Live ops re-skin whole maps: Adopt Me's 2026 Summer Camp event regraded the grass, swapped trees for pines and added docks, cabins and leaf piles across the map ([Adopt Me fandom](https://adoptme.fandom.com/wiki/Summer_Camp_Event_(2026))). All of these descriptions come from search summaries.

Our reading of these games goes beyond what the sources say. It comes from familiarity with them up to mid-2026, not from measured screenshots. The shared look has a high midday sun, a saturated blue sky with white clouds, little visible fog, and huge flat ground planes in light lime greens. The environment is a neutral stage with modest detail, and the color budget goes to the things players collect, along with their rarity glows. One central spine pulls the eye: a conveyor, a hub or a corridor. Signage is big and chunky with outlines, and frequent event re-palettes make the map look new each week without new geometry.

99 Nights in the Forest is the counterexample: muted colors "accented with occasional bursts of vibrancy" ([dr-gamer](https://dr-gamer.com/99-nights-in-the-forest)). Its warm-campfire-in-cool-dark look suits a Hood night event, not the default day. The implication for Hood is that a vibrant map does not mean every surface at maximum saturation. It means a light-valued stage, candy facades at medium-high saturation, and full saturation plus glow kept for interactables and rewards. No hit game publishes its palette or lighting values, so none of this is measured.

### Five lighting changes lift the Block's beige veil

Hood's dull look comes mostly from its lighting code, not its palette. `BlockBuilder.SetLighting()` does five things that work against vibrancy. It sets a beige Atmosphere color (218,201,172) at Haze 1.6 with an Offset of only 0.12, which washes every distant facade toward tan. It applies a ColorCorrection Saturation of −0.05. It fills shadows with a neutral gray OutdoorAmbient of (145,149,153), so shaded brick turns muddy. Its ClockTime of 17.2 gives an orange low sun that pulls reds, greens and blues toward brown. And it runs Bloom at Intensity 0.18 with Threshold 1.2, so the map's 11 Neon parts barely glow. `Config/Maps.lua` then sets the Block's Haze to 1.7 on top.

The base palette is not the problem. `TheBlockV2.lua` already uses a lawn green of (142,214,74), bricks around (204,108,72) and bright accents, built from 85 SmoothPlastic parts, 49 Metal and only 11 Neon. The team's own Steal-an-Egg build already uses a brighter recipe: ClockTime 10.5, Saturation 0.25, Bloom 0.6/28/1.1 and Atmosphere Offset 0.6 (`steal-an-egg/EggGame.server.lua`, lines 390–412).

Roblox's official samples point the same way. Island Jump, the bright stylized curriculum sample, uses a cool-blue OutdoorAmbient of (134,158,190) and a ColorShift_Top of (196,222,255) for "sharper shadows, and stronger directional lighting" ([Customize global lighting](https://create.roblox.com/docs/tutorials/curriculums/core/building/customize-global-lighting)). The stylized Laser Tag sample keeps an Atmosphere Offset of 0.65 and uses Neon floor strips in mint and pink team colors ([Construct your world](https://create.roblox.com/docs/tutorials/curriculums/environmental-art/construct-your-world)). For evening scenes, Roblox sets both ambients to a "light purple hue" of (156,136,176) ([Enhance outdoor environments](https://create.roblox.com/docs/tutorials/use-case-tutorials/lighting/enhance-outdoor-environments)). The Default tonemapper "provides vivid colors and high contrasts", while Retro makes colors "less saturated" ([Post-processing effects](https://create.roblox.com/docs/environment/post-processing-effects)). Color theory explains why a cool fill matters: value reads first, and "shadows lean cool (purple) while highlights lean warm (yellow)" ([nastyrodent](https://nastyrodent.com/color-theory-for-game-art/)).

### Candy facades, complementary doors and a 60-30-10 saturation budget

Stylized games make cities vibrant with painted color, not grime. Splatoon drew on "urban art, comic books, and street culture", with "neon pinks, greens, blues, and yellows" and complementary pairs that make colors "easier… to distinguish" ([Nintendo](https://www.nintendo.com/en-ca/whatsnew/ask-the-developer-vol-22-splatoon-raiders-part-2/)). Jet Set Radio filled "neon-lit… cityscapes" with "graffiti-layered walls" in bright cel-shaded palettes during an era of gritty realism ([Game Rant](https://gamerant.com/jet-set-radio-25-year-anniversary-art-graphics-influence/)). Subway Surfers' graffiti uses "yellow, orange, and blue, with black outlines" ([Sketchfab](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/sub-way-surf-assets-e435b57e2d7f459182b4af0fc3e38540)). Fortnite's art team turned "drab, monotone environments" into "bright and colorful set pieces", after finding that simplified realistic textures stayed "too noisy" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/art/video-developing-the-stylized-art-of-i-fortnite-i-)). Its palette is credited with staying legible "across compressed streaming thumbnails and small mobile screens" ([PSU](https://www.psu.com/news/the-secret-genius-of-fortnites-art-style/)). On Roblox, Livetopia draws players with "brighter and more colorful visuals" than Brookhaven, including "cake-style homes" and "dual-colors" ([Roblox Cities fandom](https://robloxcities.fandom.com/wiki/Livetopia)). We converted the community Brawl Stars palette to HSV: its working hues sit at 61–100% saturation and 73–100% value, and only its browns, slate and gray drop lower, serving as rest colors ([Lospec](https://lospec.com/palette-list/brawl-stars)). Da Hood-style Roblox games lean gritty, so a candy-colored hood is a differentiation bet, not a proven genre norm.

The rule that keeps this from becoming a rainbow mess is a saturation budget by screen share. We synthesized it from the value-first principle and the Brawl Stars spread.

| Layer | Share of screen | Saturation | Value | Hood examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stage and neutrals | ~60% | 0–25% | 75–100% (asphalt about 40%) | Sidewalk, curb, sky horizon, trim |
| Facades and nature | ~30% | 40–70% | 75–100% | Painted brick, lawns, trees |
| Accents and interactables | ~10% | 75–100% | 95–100%, often Neon | Signs, gym gear, cash, **tier props**, doors |

The tier ladder lives in that last 10%. If the facades climb to full saturation, the bags stop standing out.

The proposed palettes below refine the earlier report's hex table. Sidewalks get lighter (#EDE6DA instead of #CFC8B8) and asphalt bluer (#4B5263 instead of #4A4E5C), so the stage reads clean.

Three placement rules complete the system. Shadows are never gray: their color comes from OutdoorAmbient, so the cool H1 fill turns the shaded side of a red brick wall a rich purple-red instead of muddy brown. Near-black is kept for small details such as outlines, tires, window frames and ring posts, because a large dark roof or road eats vibrancy. And Neon grows from 11 parts to a target of 40–80, all at destinations: a rotating magenta-and-cyan glove sign on the gym, a lime-and-magenta "DRIP" sign on the clothing shop, a marquee over each next stage, and the tier trims inside the gym. Each map tier keeps one hero hue, as Pet Simulator 99's zones do. The Block's gold-orange (242,182,50) is already in `Maps.lua`, alongside green for the Suburbs, blue for Uptown and orange for the Hills, and it should tint signage, bunting and ColorShift_Top. The long street of stage walls becomes a mural gallery. Each stage gets its own dominant hue and a giant white stage number with a black outline, Steal an Egg's biome logic in paint.

### Motion at destinations, not clutter everywhere, makes the street feel alive

Hits feel alive through motion and color on what players care about, not through props on every surface. Roblox's Laser Tag sample adds life with three cheap layers: dynamic clouds, foreground cloud particles (FacingCameraWorldUp, Size 100, Squash −0.25) and floating dust ([Construct your world](https://create.roblox.com/docs/tutorials/curriculums/environmental-art/construct-your-world)). Dynamic clouds drift with `Workspace.GlobalWind` ([Dynamic clouds](https://create.roblox.com/docs/environment/clouds); [Global wind](https://create.roblox.com/docs/environment/global-wind)). For the Block, we propose a client-side motion budget in four groups. Collectibles move constantly: cash and coins bob ±0.5 studs over about 1.2 seconds and spin about 90° per second, and tier trims pulse. Three to five animated landmarks stay in view at once, such as the rotating gym sign, a barber pole, an open fire hydrant spraying light-blue droplets, chasing marquee bulbs and swaying bunting. Ambience fills the gaps with drifting clouds, one to three pigeons, swaying laundry lines, leaves near the park at Rate 2–4 and looping cars in saturated paint. The eight existing neighbors wear outfits matching their house's accent color. Sound counts toward "alive" too, even though it never shows in screenshots: a boombox by the court, the gym's speed-bag rattle, birds by day, crickets at dusk and an ice-cream-truck jingle.

Density works best in nodes. Each stoop, shop front, corner, court and gym entrance gets three to six props, 40–50% of the visible ground stays plain, light surface, and a repeating rhythm keeps it orderly: a planter on every stoop, an awning on every second house, a mural or flag on every third. The eye should be led to destinations, so the brightest, most saturated objects sit at the gym door, the shop sign and the next stage gate, lane-yellow arrows on the sidewalk point to the next goal, and bunting and string lights strung across the street pull the eye down its axis. One test catches most mistakes: take a phone-sized screenshot and squint at it or convert it to grayscale. If the gym, the shop and the next stage don't jump out instantly, demote the competing props by lowering their saturation or raising their value.

Event re-palettes then become cheap. A Block Party night uses H2. A Summer event adds hydrant sprays and popsicle colors. A Winter event adds snow caps, warm windows and string lights. Each swaps the lighting and a handful of props rather than the map.

### Authentic thumbnails require a vibrant map, because grading the capture breaks policy

Roblox's icon guidance links "bright, high-saturation colors" to fantasy and fun, and muted colors to somber settings ([Experience icons](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/publishing/experience-icons)). Its thumbnail rules call for 16:9 at 1920×1080, warn against essential elements at the bottom where player counts can cover them, and cite a "comical theme/character with bright colors" as a positive example. Video thumbnails allow "minor post-processing" but reject "**Artificial Visuals**": graphics must be "representative of the actual in-game visuals" ([Thumbnails](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/publishing/thumbnails)). Third-party 2026 advice adds that "muted, realistic palettes disappear" in the feed. It recommends a single subject filling a third of the frame with a rim light or outline, and three words or fewer ([vizzbees](https://vizzbees.com/blog/how-to-make-a-roblox-thumbnail)).

A dull map therefore can't be rescued in the capture. Hood should build two or three thumbnail spots that players can reach in normal play. One frames a player in front of the neon gym sign with coral rowhouses and blue sky behind. Another looks down the bunting axis during a Block Party. Captures use H1 or H2, keep the hero in the upper two-thirds and add no more than about +0.05 saturation at capture time. No Roblox data links map saturation to click-through rate, so this rests on policy and principle, not measured lift.

## Trial build: the ladder, the effects and the relit Block in The Block V2

### The nine stations were built from parts, and five fixes made them read as props

All nine stations now exist as code in `hood/src/ServerStorage/HoodProps.lua`, and The Block V2 places them in the club and the ring. Each station follows the ladder above. The Tire Bag sits on a cracked slab with a wooden gallows. The Duct Tape and Street bags move to plywood and curb bases. The Heavy Bag adds the first light, a purple uplight. The Speed Bag adds the first particles, and the Double-End Bag adds the first moving part, an orbit ring with neon bungees and lightning beams. The Pro Bag adds embers, a spotlight and a spinning floor emblem. The Gold Bag gets a marble dais, a god-ray beam, a bobbing halo and gem sparkles. The Champ Ring adds hue-cycling ropes, a belt hologram and confetti. Every station shares the same nameplate, star count and price label, so only the prop itself escalates.

Five problems only showed up in renders, and each fix is now a rule in the builder. Curved parts that intersect show a jagged seam, so bags are built as stacked barrel sections with the seam bands sitting exactly on the steps. Gold Foil read as brown under daylight, so gold uses SmoothPlastic in a warm yellow, with Neon only on thin trims. Diamonds the size of ice cubes looked like ice cubes, so the gems became small neon chips set into the seam bands. Emitters placed at a bag's center emitted inside it and were invisible, so every aura emits from an invisible shell slightly larger than the prop. A chain hanging straight down produced NaN rotations, so the chain helper now passes an explicit up vector.

### Hit effects reuse their instances, and better bags hit a little harder

The `Juice` module no longer creates parts or Highlights per punch. Bursts come from a pool of six anchor parts that are reused in turn, and since particles live in world space, moving an anchor never drags the previous burst along. Each bag model keeps one `HitFlash` Highlight that is enabled for the 0.12-second flash and disabled afterwards, so it only counts toward the 255-Highlight limit while it is visible. `Juice.punch` now accepts the station's tier and rarity color: sparks take the bag's color and grow about 6% per tier, so the Gold Bag's hits feel heavier than the Tire Bag's without adding particles.

### Candy facades, doors and destination neon lifted the street, and an overlap check kept it clean

The vibrancy pass followed the color budget above. Lobby facades are colored by graph coloring so that no two touching blocks share a color, with every third block left as brick. Each corridor segment between stage gates takes one candy hue, and higher terrace tiers are darkened by about 8% per step so the setbacks still read. Street-level walls get doors with stoops, striped awnings, complementary-colored doors and planters. Windows get flower boxes, and grass lips carry a fringe of flowers. Neon is reserved for destinations: marquee bulbs and bunting at the stage gates, a spinning cyan ring on the giant glove, a lime-and-magenta DRIP blade sign on the shop's street corner, and the stations themselves. A half basketball court with an orange floor, a blue key and a full hoop now sits on the east lawn, with a few leaves drifting down over it at Rate 3. It is the landmark and thumbnail spot this report recommends.

The overlap check caught what the renders missed. A script compares every pair of coplanar, same-facing faces in the exported map and flags any with different colors or materials, since those flicker in Studio. Comparing it against the build from before the pass found sixteen new pairs. The visible ones were trims whose top face sat exactly level with the block underneath (now 0.02–0.04 studs lower), and doors and flower boxes buried in the station platform at the end of the street. All of those were fixed, and doors and street-level flower boxes now share one avoid-list. Listing every part around the new court also caught a bench clipping the subway railing, which moved. The five pairs left are faces buried inside other parts, such as the inner sides of the round dais segments and rope ends inside the ring's corner pads. The renderer approximates Roblox lighting but doesn't apply the color grade, so the `Day` preset in `HoodLighting.lua` should make the street more saturated in Studio than in these previews.

## Conclusion

Taken together, the three requests describe one color economy. Each station has a single tier color, and it should drive the trim, nameplate, price label, particle ramp, light color and Highlight outline. That color only reads because the map's 60-30-10 budget leaves the accent layer empty for it. The earlier report suggested one shared Theme module, and this research now fills it: station colors in `Skins.lua` corrected to the ladder, a `BagFX` table of layers per tier, H1 and H2 lighting presets in `Maps.lua`, and the facade and neon palettes. With those in data, a new tier, a weekly re-palette or an event skin is an edit rather than a rebuild, which is what the earlier report's retention findings require. Tier escalation also works as a retention feature, not just polish. A visible staircase of locked silhouettes, a single teasing next bag and server-wide unlock announcements make progress visible to other players, the trait the earlier report linked to return visits.

The other lesson is to design for a phone with no bloom. Neon glow and sun shadows disappear at low quality, additive particles wash out against the brighter H1 daylight, and flipbooks switch off on low-memory phones. Every tier must still be distinguishable through solid color, value, outline, pedestal height and motion; glow is a bonus for capable devices. That sets the order of work: the five lighting changes first, because they take minutes; then the `Juice` churn fixes (a pooled rig and persistent Highlights); then unified station colors; then the parts-first prop ladder; and a mesh pipeline only once someone confirms that uploaded meshes avoid the EditableMesh verification toggle. Several things remain unknown: no hit publishes its lighting values, no screenshots of real tier ladders were available, nobody has researched how sound should escalate by tier, which locked-state style converts best is untested, and it is unclear whether graphics quality still throttles particle rates in late 2026.
