# Timing, not textures, makes Roblox effects pop

The hit Roblox games (The Strongest Battlegrounds, Jujutsu Shenanigans, Blox Fruits, Pet Simulator 99 and the "+1" simulators) do not publish their VFX or animation settings, and this research found **no first-party breakdown from any of those studios** and no measured frame data for their moves. What can be documented is the shared pipeline they all sit on, plus the craft rules that separate pro work from cheap work, and the two together are enough to reproduce the quality without copying anything. The pipeline is: hand-made flipbook sprite sheets on ParticleEmitters, low-poly Blender meshes (crescents, rings, domes) with scrolling textures, Beams and Trails for streaks, an emitter plugin that fires layers on a timeline, and every effect triggered from an animation marker on the exact contact frame. The single biggest difference between cheap and pro is **timing**: pros finish the main hit in the **first quarter of an effect's life** and never use linear fades, while amateurs use one long, even, linear fade on uniform particles ([VFX Apprentice](https://www.vfxapprentice.com/blog/creating-vfx-style-guides-for-games)). Character animation in October 2026 is still hand-keyed: **Roblox Studio has no text-to-animation**, and mocap gives floaty, realistic timing that has to be re-timed into snappy anime poses ([NPCMotion](https://www.npcmotion.com/blog/roblox-ai-tools)). For a cannon shot, the recipe is a slow anticipation against an almost instant release and recoil, a springy return, layered muzzle effects from fastest to slowest, a trauma-based camera shake opposite the shot, and a landing that mirrors the launch. Throughout this report, **cited facts** carry a link, and **suggested starting values** (our own inferences, to be tuned on a 30 fps capture next to a reference) are labelled as such.

## The hits share a pipeline, not secret settings

Every pro effect on Roblox is built from five engine parts, and each has documented limits that shape the work. **Flipbooks** are the core of hand-drawn anime smoke, fire and slashes. They left beta on Oct 21, 2022, with layouts `Grid2x2`, `Grid4x4` and `Grid8x8`, and the texture must be **square and power-of-two, from 8 to 1024 px**, with spacing around frames to avoid artifacts ([DevForum: Particles' Flipbook Release](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/particles’-flipbook-release/2029388)). On a 1024 sheet, 2x2 gives 4 frames of 512 px, 4x4 gives **16 frames of 256 px**, and 8x8 gives **64 frames of 128 px**; to fit a longer animation, drop every other frame ([DevForum: Flipbooks Beta](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/animated-particles-with-the-particle-flipbooks-beta/1718023)). `FlipbookFramerate` tops out at **30 fps**, and a `Custom` layout using `FlipbookSizeX/Y` now exists ([Creator Docs: Particle emitters](https://create.roblox.com/docs/effects/particle-emitters)), backed by a client beta for custom layouts ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/client-beta-optimize-your-particle-animations-with-custom-flipbook-layouts/4005128)). The modes matter: **OneShot** plays once over the particle's Lifetime and ignores the framerate, Loop cycles, PingPong bounces, and Random crossfades frames for slow twinkles; `FlipbookStartRandom` with framerate 0 turns one 8x8 sheet into **64 different static shapes**, an easy way to get varied debris and puffs from one emitter ([DevForum: Flipbooks Beta](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/animated-particles-with-the-particle-flipbooks-beta/1718023)). Roblox staff packed frames with Free Texture Packer and similar tools at a fixed 1024x1024 with **trim and rotate off** ([same](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/animated-particles-with-the-particle-flipbooks-beta/1718023)). Flipbooks cost memory, low-memory phones may turn them off, and reusing one texture across emitters is cheaper than many unique ones ([Creator Docs](https://create.roblox.com/docs/effects/particle-emitters)). Users also report a visible crossfade between frames on cartoony sprites that Roblox has not addressed ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/particles’-flipbook-release/2029388)).

**Mesh VFX** carry the shapes particles cannot: slashes, rings, shockwave domes and wind spirals. The documented crescent recipe is a squashed sphere unwrapped along a seam in Blender, with streaks painted in GIMP and bent into an arc by the Polar Coordinates filter. The texture goes on two `Texture` objects inside the mesh rather than in TextureID, so the mesh can fade ([DevForum: Crescent Sword Slash](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/crecent-sword-slash-effect/1113871)). Scripts scroll those textures through `OffsetStudsU/V` ([DevForum: Texture Offsets](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/texture-offsets-2019/314381/59)), and the ForceField material's Fresnel rim, shaped with Reflectance, makes shields and domes ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/control-the-fresnel-effect-of-forcefield-material-using-the-reflection-property/258289)). **Beams** are textured Bézier ribbons between two Attachments: `CurveSize0/1` bend them into slash arcs, and Wrap mode repeats the texture by length while Stretch repeats it a fixed number of times ([Creator Docs: Beams](https://create.roblox.com/docs/effects/beams)). **Trails** draw between two attachments and take Lifetime, WidthScale, Transparency and Color sequences, FaceCamera and LightEmission ([Creator Docs: Trail](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/Trail)).

What turns these parts into a pro workflow in 2025–2026 is the **emitter plugin**. VFX Forge puts `EmitCount`, `EmitDelay` and `EmitDuration` attributes on each emitter, with curve editors for Transparency, Size, Squash and TimeScale ([Forge docs](https://zilibobi.github.io/forge-docs/effects/particle)). The Sparkle/VFXer plugin adds `EmitRepeat`, `ResetOnFinish`, tween-in/out for lights and a one-click `Color` attribute ([VFXer attributes](https://vfxer.gitbook.io/plugin/attributes)). The newest, **Sine VFX** (Aug 2026, $5), adds mesh "3D particles", bezier paths, craters, trail lightning and a particle counter, and its author says it was built with AI help ([DevForum: Sine VFX](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/plugin-sine-vfx-a-vfx-plugin-for-roblox-studio/4803771)). These attributes let one `Emit` call fire a whole timeline (flash now, sparks at 0.02 s, smoke at 0.05 s), which is how a single effect gets its layers. A suggested house rule: adopt the same `EmitDelay/EmitCount/EmitDuration` attribute convention in our own runtime module. Effects then become data the owner can tweak in Studio's Properties panel, and Claude can edit them without touching code.

The last piece is **networking**. A developer who built a TSB/JJS-style game described the pattern in Aug 2026: the client sends input, the server validates state and cooldowns, the server tells all clients, and **each client plays the animation locally and fires VFX and sound from animation events**. That stays smooth even when the attacker lags, and most skills don't need unreliable remotes ([DevForum: Battlegrounds VFX replication](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/best-way-to-replicate-vfxanimations-for-a-battlegrounds-game-tsbjjs-style-architecture/4814813)). The long-running "Client Replication 101" guide adds the rules: send minimal data (an origin CFrame and parameters), let the caster play their own effect instantly, **"ONLY REPLICATE VFX NOT HITBOXES"**, send only to players in range, and use `workspace:GetServerTimeNow()` timestamps for late joiners ([DevForum: Client Replication 101](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/client-replication-101-the-guide-to-replicating-effects-to-clients/1789487)). Rate-limit VFX remotes, because exploiters spam them ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/how-should-i-prevent-exploiters-spamming-my-remoteevent-for-vfx-replication/4749010)).

Engine limits bound all of this: particle **Lifetime is capped at 20 s**, Rate at **400 per second per emitter (100 on mobile)**, `LightEmission` 1 is additive glow and 0 is normal blending, and `ZOffset` pushes a layer forward without resizing it ([Creator Docs](https://create.roblox.com/docs/effects/particle-emitters)). `Emit(count)` defaults to 16 ([Creator Docs: ParticleEmitter](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/ParticleEmitter)).

| Production step | Cited fact | Suggested starting value (ours, tune by eye) |
|---|---|---|
| Flipbook sheet | Square power-of-two, max 1024, 30 fps cap, spacing between frames | 1024 px Grid4x4 (16 frames of 256 px, sharp on the owner's Retina Mac); 4–8 px empty margin inside each cell |
| Burst flipbook | OneShot plays over Lifetime | Lifetime 0.3–0.6 s, so 16 frames run near the 30 fps cap |
| Making frames | Packers at fixed 1024, trim and rotate off | Claude draws frames in Python (PIL) or Blender and packs them by script; the owner uploads the PNG and sends back the ID |
| Mesh library | Crescent via squashed sphere and Polar Coordinates | Build once in Blender: crescent, flat ring, open dome, helix ribbon, spike cone, low-poly sphere, each UV'd as one straight strip |
| Mesh timing | No source gives numbers | Scale-up 0.1–0.2 s Quad/Expo Out, fade 0.3–0.6 s Quad In on the Texture objects, slight spin |
| Energy beam | Wrap repeats by length | Stack 2–3 Beams (dark edge, coloured body, white core) with scrolling TextureSpeed |
| Weapon trail | Trail properties documented, defaults not fetched | Lifetime 0.15–0.3 s, WidthScale tapering to 0, LightEmission about 1 |

## Cheap effects fail on timing first, then shape, then colour

The most reliable guidance on why effects look cheap comes from professional VFX teaching, and its first rule is about time, not art. Pro effects have three beats: a **wind-up, a climax and a fall-off**. Instant effects such as a hit open at full intensity, and the impact must land **on the exact frame damage is dealt** ([VFX Apprentice: Timing](https://www.vfxapprentice.com/blog/the-soul-of-effects-what-is-timing-in-vfx)). The style-guide rule is blunt: **"finish the main action within the first quarter of the effect's lifetime, then dissipate gradually"** and **"avoid linear timing wherever possible"** ([VFX Apprentice: Style Guides](https://www.vfxapprentice.com/blog/creating-vfx-style-guides-for-games)). Calm and healing effects should pulse **under 60 BPM**, because pacing faster than a heartbeat reads as aggressive ([VFX Apprentice: Timing](https://www.vfxapprentice.com/blog/the-soul-of-effects-what-is-timing-in-vfx)). For a kid game this gives a clean split: hits and launches snap, while rewards, auras and pets breathe slower than once per second.

Shape comes second. Pro shapes are "large, simple, while incorporating **thick and thin lines** for balance", hand-painted, and never "overly detailed" or realistic ([VFX Apprentice: Style Guides](https://www.vfxapprentice.com/blog/creating-vfx-style-guides-for-games)). Colour comes third: keep effect saturation and value **above the characters but slightly below the UI**, limit the palette to analogous or triadic hues, and "crunch" gradients in the highlights rather than blending them, because blended highlights go muddy ([same](https://www.vfxapprentice.com/blog/creating-vfx-style-guides-for-games)). A DevForum critique of a dull star effect asked for exactly these fixes: a glowing core with **small, very bright bits**, plus **debris shooting out from the centre at high speed** ([DevForum: Is this to much?](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/is-this-to-much-vfx/4762194)).

Roblox adds one trap of its own: additive blending. At `LightEmission` 1 overlapping particles keep adding up, so stacked layers turn to **white fog on a bright sky**, and because additive black adds nothing, **dark particles at LightEmission 1 vanish** ([DevForum: LightEmission black](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/when-coloring-particles-with-lightemission-1-to-black-they-become-transparent/2614320)). `Brightness` only works once `LightInfluence` is below 1 ([DevForum: Introduction to VFX](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/introduction-to-vfx-particles/2068650)). Uniform particles are the other giveaway: `Lifetime`, `Rotation` and `RotSpeed` take ranges, and `Size` and `Transparency` curves take per-keypoint envelopes for random variation ([same](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/introduction-to-vfx-particles/2068650)). The docs sample starts each particle at a random 0–360° rotation and fades transparency in and back out rather than popping ([Creator Docs: ParticleEmitter](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/ParticleEmitter)).

The table below turns each tell into a fix. The cause and the cited mechanism come from the sources above. Rows marked "conv." are widely taught practice that this research could not tie to a fetched page.

| Tell | Cause | Roblox fix |
|---|---|---|
| Mushy, floaty burst | One long Lifetime, linear fade | `Emit()` for hits instead of Rate; burst Lifetime range about 0.15–0.4 s (ours); peak inside the first 25% of life (cited) |
| Clone or wallpaper look | Every particle identical | Rotation (-180, 180), RotSpeed range, Lifetime range, Size envelope, `FlipbookStartRandom` (cited properties) |
| "Default Roblox" look | Built-in sparkle and fire textures | Our own thick-and-thin hand-painted shapes on 4x4 flipbooks with padding |
| White fog in daylight | Stacked additive layers | Additive only on the small hot core; body and smoke at LightEmission 0–0.3 (conv.) |
| Dark smoke disappears | Dark colour on an additive layer | Dark layers at LightEmission 0 (cited mechanism) |
| Flat, no punch | No value contrast | Bright tiny core, saturated middle, darker rim; check the effect in greyscale (conv.) |
| Rainbow mush | Too many hues | Analogous palette with one accent (cited); the accent is the skin's colour, per `VFX-ITEMS.md` |
| Weightless hit | No secondary layers | Fast debris outward (cited critique), lingering ground dust ring (conv.) |
| Effect drifts off the move | Fired from Play() or a timer | Fire from an animation marker on the contact frame (cited principle) |
| Looping aura pops | Seam in the loop | Seamless sheet on FlipbookMode Loop; fade in and out at both ends (cited docs sample) |

Practitioner convention, not measured data, puts a white flash at **1–3 frames** (0.03–0.1 s at 30 fps), impact sparks at 0.1–0.4 s, a shockwave ring at 0.2–0.35 s, dust lingering 0.5–1.5 s, and a whole hit at **1 s or less**. Those fit the cited first-quarter rule. Under the house rule of **at most one full-screen flash per second**, even a 1-frame flash counts, so a flash gets one per hit and repeated hits must be rate-limited. A safer default for kids is to keep the flash local, as a bright particle at the impact point, and skip the screen-wide flash entirely.

Animation has its own cheap tells, and the Roblox docs and long-standing community guides name them. The Animation Editor offers only **Linear, Constant, CubicV2, Elastic and Bounce** easing, with In, Out and InOut directions, at **30 fps** ([Creator Docs: Animation Editor](https://create.roblox.com/docs/animation/editor)). An all-linear clip "will look like a robot did it" ([DevForum: Jespone's guide](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/jespones-guide-to-animations/225752)). The top beginner mistake is **twinning**: every limb hitting its key on the same frame in a straight line. The fix is key poses first, then in-betweens with mixed easing and staggered timing, follow-through on every part except the root, and poses pushed past realistic ([same](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/jespones-guide-to-animations/225752)). Real motion is never constant: a landing leg decelerates and then "kicks back with acceleration in an explosive manner" ([DevForum: natural animations](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/making-animations-feel-more-natural-and-human/2736367)). Two engine traps finish the list. Loops do not blend the last key back to the first, so **the first key must be duplicated as the last**. Attacks set to Core or Idle priority get overridden by walk and idle, which makes them look limp ([Creator Docs: Animation Editor](https://create.roblox.com/docs/animation/editor)). As suggested defaults: offset head, arms and torso by 1–3 frames; hold extremes as a "moving hold" of 2–6 frames with tiny drift rather than a freeze; and show a swing too fast for the eye with a Trail or slash mesh on the strike frames.

## Snappy characters are still hand-keyed in October 2026

The 2026 animation toolset has moved, but it has not replaced the animator. Roblox's **September 2025 Animation Clip Editor update** made the editor preview evaluate keys exactly like the engine, fixed Auto Tangent bounce and 0→180° rotation display, and exposed a long-standing bug: **legacy Cubic easing had In and Out swapped at runtime**, so old "Cubic V1" clips keep the old behaviour while **CubicV2** behaves as labelled ([DevForum: Clip Editor Update](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/animation-clip-editor-update-better-authoring-playback/3956668)). New clips should use CubicV2, and old Cubic clips may ease the "wrong" way when edited. A Curve Editor gives per-channel tangent control ([DevForum: Curve Editor](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/new-animation-curve-editor-beta-fine-grain-control-of-animations/1770367)). Roblox's built-in **Animation Capture** (beta) turns a single-person, well-lit, stable-camera video **under 15 s into editable R15 keyframes** in about a minute; it works on **R15 only** ([Creator Docs: Animation capture](https://create.roblox.com/docs/animation/capture)).

AI has not reached character animation inside Studio. An October 6, 2026 guide reports that the Roblox Assistant has no animation command and **"There is no text-to-animation in Studio"**. The RDC 2026 roadmap points to prompt-set NPC behaviour and motion matching for default movement in late 2026–2027. That guide comes from a vendor selling a third-party text-to-R15 plugin, so treat it with care ([NPCMotion](https://www.npcmotion.com/blog/roblox-ai-tools)). Roblox's Cube "4D" model generates functional objects, lists animation as something it "will eventually" do, and one reviewer called it "in its infancy" ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/roblox/4d-generative-ai-model-update); [Roblox Newsroom](https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2026/02/accelerating-creation-powered-roblox-cube-foundation-model)). Outside Studio, video mocap is cheap. Rokoko's free tier allows unlimited FBX export but only **30 s a month** of video capture, with paid tiers from about $10 ([Rokoko Pricing](https://www.rokoko.com/pricing)). DeepMotion runs from $9 a month with 60 free seconds, and Cascadeur offers physics-assisted posing free for non-commercial use ([fast.io, aggregator](https://fast.io/resources/best-ai-animation-tools-2026/)). Blender-to-Roblox FBX export has documented settings: **Add Leaf Bones off**, Apply Scalings set to FBX Unit Scale, and Simplify 0.0 ([Creator Docs: Export from Blender](https://create.roblox.com/docs/it-it/avatar/emotes/export)).

**Moon Animator 2** remains the battlegrounds standard. Animators insert an R6 rig, rotate parts with auto-keying, use the marker track for hit events, and use a camera track with FOV keys for cutscenes. The guide warns to avoid linear easing except for arcs and ragdoll ([DevForum: Moon Animator 2](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/getting-started-with-moon-animator-2-unofficial/476330)). Moon's camera and effect tracks don't export as a Roblox animation and need custom code to replay in-game ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/how-do-i-play-moon-2-files-effects-and-camera-movement/2878483)). Combat developers prefer R6 because it is "easier to animate, looks better", an R15 cutscene takes about **2.5× longer**, and adjustable R15 proportions can shrink some players' hitboxes, though Heroes Battlegrounds shows skilled R6 work can rival anything ([DevForum: Why R6](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/why-do-combat-game-developers-lean-more-towards-r6-rather-than-r15/3136549)). For a kid game on standard avatars, R15 is the practical choice, since it is the default and Animation Capture needs it. Get R6's boldness by leaning and twisting the split torso hard rather than bending small limbs.

The frame numbers that make anime combat feel snappy are not published for TSB or JJS. The best available numbers come from general fighting-game practice and from the LihFolk vault in this repo, which measured one live action game. A typical 3D fighting-game jab connects around **frame 8–10 at 60 fps**, about 4–5 frames at 30 fps ([Pushing Buttons](https://pushing-buttons.blogspot.com/2009/02/attack-animations-part-2.html)). The vault and the repo's adopted chart give the rest, cited to repo files rather than the web.

| Move (30 fps) | Wind-up | Strike | Hold | Recovery | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Light M1 jab | 2–3 f | 2 f | 1–2 f | 4–6 f (≈0.35 s per hit) | repo: `Build Playbooks/15 Anime VFX and Animation Playbook.md` §6 |
| M1 finisher | 5–6 f | 3 f | 3 f | 8–10 f | same |
| Heavy | 8–12 f | 3–4 f | 3 f | 10–14 f | same |
| Ultimate | 15–30 f charge | n/a | n/a | n/a | same |
| Landing | 2–4 f down | n/a | n/a | 4–8 f | repo: `LihFolk/.../Timing Spacing and Frame Charts.md` |

The same vault notes give the craft rules around those charts. **Hit-stop belongs in code at runtime, never baked into the clip**: 30–60 ms for light hits and 80–150 ms for heavies, applied only on confirmed hits so a whiff never looks like a hit. For networked games, slow the track to about 0.12× and then catch up at about 1.88× rather than freezing it, so the body stays in step with clock-timed sparks (repo: `LihFolk/LihFolk Vault V2/08 Animation/05 Systems/Hit Reactions Stagger and Hit-Stop.md`). A blade at impact moves 1,000–2,000° per second, so at 1–2 strike frames **the eye sees nothing**, and the path must be shown with a trail or smear mesh switched on and off by markers (repo: `.../Timing Spacing and Frame Charts.md`). Priority works per joint: an attack that keys only the upper body lets the legs keep walking, while one with leg keys makes the character skate, so ship `<Clip>Upper` copies for moving attacks (repo: `.../Playback, Priorities and Blending.md`). The community's standard pose freeze is `AdjustSpeed(0)` ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/how-to-freeze-animation-in-current-time/1295624)), and AdjustSpeed scales marker times too. The recommended pipeline follows: record a 5–15 s reference video; capture it (R15) or use it as reference; cut it down to 4–8 key poses; push the poses; re-time to the chart; add `Hit`, `TrailOn` and `TrailOff` markers; then publish. Budget most of the time for re-timing, not capture.

## A cannon shot sells itself in the first 0.1 seconds

No source breaks down the launch of +1 Stone Skipping or any other Roblox launch simulator. The game is a popular "+1" throw sim (place 10765298801), but no one has published its animation or camera ([RoWatcher](https://rowatcher.com/games/10765298801/1-stone-skipping)). What follows is a recipe built from cited principles, with every number marked as a suggested starting value. Those principles are the classic animation rules. **Squash and stretch keep volume constant**, **anticipation** prepares the action, loose parts keep moving after the body stops (**follow-through**), motion clusters frames at the start and end (**slow in, slow out**), and thrown objects travel in **parabolic arcs** ([Twelve principles](https://cavac.at/cavacopedia/Twelve%20basic%20principles%20of%20animation)). "Juice It or Lose It" (GDC Europe 2012) showed tweening and particles turning a dull Breakout clone satisfying live on stage ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1016487/Juice-It-or-Lose)). GDC later hosted a counter-talk, "Don't Juice It or Lose It", a reminder that juice can be overdone ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1020861/Don-t-Juice-It-or)). For springy motion, the MIT-licensed **spr** library animates any property with a physical spring: damping below 1 overshoots and wobbles, exactly 1 settles cleanly, and its examples use `(1, 1)` for slow and clean and `(0.6, 4)` for quick and bouncy ([spr GitHub](https://github.com/Fraktality/spr)). A Model has no tweenable CFrame, so the standard approach (Roblox practice, not cited here) is to tween or spring a `CFrameValue` and call `model:PivotTo()` on each change. A rigged cannon (Motor6D joints played through an `Animator` under an `AnimationController`) can instead use an authored "Fire" clip, since the controller's own `LoadAnimation` is deprecated in favour of the Animator ([Creator Docs: AnimationController](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/AnimationController)).

The contrast between a slow charge and an almost instant release is what creates punch. A cheap launch tweens everything at one even speed. All values in the table are suggested starting points.

| Phase (suggested) | Time | Motion | Easing |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Charge | 0.25–0.45 s | Barrel tips up 4–8°, body squashes (X/Z 1.08–1.12, Y 0.88–0.92), creeps back 0.1–0.2 studs; ±0.5° tremble in the last 0.1 s | Quad or Sine InOut |
| B. Release | 1–2 frames (≈0.03 s) | Snap-stretch along the barrel: length 1.15–1.25, width 0.9 | Instant set or Exponential Out |
| C. Recoil kick | 0.05–0.08 s | Slides back about 0.15–0.25 × barrel length, pitches up another 6–12° | Exponential or Quint Out |
| D. Return | 0.25–0.45 s | Returns and overshoots forward 5–10% of the kick, then settles | Back Out, or spr damping 0.4–0.55, frequency 4–6 |
| E. Carriage wobble | Starts 1–2 frames after C, lasts 0.4–0.6 s | Rocks 2–4° on the rear axle; scale returns from stretch with one 0.97 overshoot | spr damping 0.3–0.5, frequency 3–5 |
| F. Idle | After about 0.6 s | 1% breathing scale so it never looks frozen | Sine InOut, 1.5–2 s period |

**Muzzle effects** layer from fastest to slowest (suggested). First a flash of 1–2 big star particles lasting 0.05–0.08 s, plus a PointLight at Brightness 4–8 and Range 12–20 that fades out in 0.08–0.12 s. Then **12–25 sparks** at Speed 20–40 with Drag 3–5 over 0.2–0.4 s. A ring mesh scales from 0.5× to 3× the bore with Exponential Out over 0.3–0.4 s while it fades. Then 6–10 soft cartoon puffs grow from size 2 to 5 over 0.8–1.2 s. Last, the barrel tip glows hot orange and cools over 0.6–1.0 s. The flash stays at the muzzle. If the screen tints at all, keep it at 0.1 s or less and 30% opacity or less, at most once per second, behind a settings toggle.

**In flight**, readability wins. A suggested setup: stretch the visual mesh (never the physics part) along its velocity by Z = 1 + clamp(speed/120, 0, 0.6), with X and Y set to 1/√Z to keep volume. Spin it 1–3 turns per second. Add a Trail one ball-width wide that tapers to nothing over 0.3–0.6 s, coloured in the skin's accent, with a thinner, brighter inner trail for a comet look. Add Velocity-Parallel speed-line particles above a speed threshold, a drop shadow, and a floating distance number.

**The camera** does as much as the cannon. The trauma model from Squirrel Eiserloh's GDC 2016 talk makes **shake intensity = trauma²**. The Bevy reference implementation uses a 10° maximum angle, **+0.4 trauma per event** and **0.5 per second decay** ([Bevy example](https://bevy.org/examples/camera/2d-screen-shake/); [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1023146/Math-for-Game-Programmers-Juicing)). Vlambeer's rule is to **shake opposite the direction of fire** ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/vlambeer-co-founder-shares-advice-on-building-better-action-games)). On Roblox, EZ Camera Shake runs at `RenderPriority.Camera.Value + 1`, and its source code defines **Bump = (magnitude 2.5, roughness 4, fade-in 0.1, fade-out 0.75)** and **Explosion = (5, 10, 0, 1.5)** ([RbxCameraShaker presets](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Sleitnick/RbxCameraShaker/master/src/CameraShaker/CameraShakePresets.lua)). A chase cam sets the camera to Scriptable and updates it every frame from the projectile ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/bind-camera-to-projectile/1861469)). Shake should come with an **on/off toggle and a slider** ([DEV: Art of Screen-shake](https://dev.to/dat_one_dev/the-art-of-screen-shake-33bi)), which the house rule makes mandatory. Suggested values: at fire, add trauma 0.35–0.5 with a pitch kick, no stronger than Bump for kids, decaying within 0.5 s, multiplied by the player's setting. Widen the FOV from 70 to 80–85 in 0.06–0.1 s, ease back to about 75 during flight, and return to 70 at landing. (70 as Roblox's default is general knowledge, not checked against a source.) Hold the cannon framing for 2–5 frames so the player sees the recoil, then hand off to a chase cam 12–18 studs behind and 4–6 studs above the ball, smoothed with alpha = 1 − exp(−k·dt) at k = 6–10. A little lag is what shows speed.

**Hit-stop** sells the landing. A general game-feel source uses **0.08 s at time scale 0.05**. It warns against an exact 0, which can break code that divides by frame time, and says to handle back-to-back hits with a counter ([Uhiyama Lab](https://uhiyama-lab.com/en/notes/godot/hit-feedback-hitstop/)). Roblox has no global time scale, so the suggested version is visual and client-only. Freeze the client's ball for 3–6 frames, drop nearby emitters' `TimeScale` to about 0.05, pause the camera follow, and play the impact sound at full speed. Scale it by tier: nothing for small shots, 0.04 s for medium, 0.08–0.12 s for record shots. **The landing mirrors the launch** (suggested values). The ball squashes to Y 0.6 and X/Z 1.3, a ground ring grows from 0.5× to 4× with Exponential Out, 8–14 dust puffs rise, and the ball unsquashes on a spring (damping 0.35–0.5, frequency 5–7). Each skip gets a smaller squash and ring and a rising "+1" pitch. The distance number pops 0 → 1.2 → 1.0 with Back Out over 0.35 s and counts up with Quad Out. For mobile, keep it to **under about 100 live particles per shot**, one PointLight, at most two Trails, and pooled projectiles. The server decides power and distance, and every client plays the visuals from one RemoteEvent.

## Conclusion

The research mostly shows that the polish of the top games is not locked away. The engine features, plugins and networking pattern are all public, and the gap between cheap and pro is mostly decisions about time: when the peak lands, how fast the release is, how long the hold lasts, and whether the effect fires on the contact frame. That suits a Claude-built studio. Timing can be specified as numbers, checked on a 30 fps capture and tuned without new art. It also means the bottleneck is not tooling but **reference calibration**. Every frame count for TSB, JJS, Blox Fruits, Pet Simulator 99, the anime TD and RNG games and +1 Stone Skipping is still unmeasured, so the next step is to record our own 30 fps captures of the moments we want to match and step through them frame by frame, rather than search for settings nobody publishes.

Three points cut against intuition. First, adding particles is the least effective fix. Shortening lifetimes, randomising ranges and moving the peak earlier do more than any new emitter, and additive layers actively hurt on a bright, cartoony sky. Second, AI and mocap speed up roughing-in but make anime motion worse until it is re-timed, so the "AI animation" win in 2026 is faster iteration on hand-keyed poses, not generated clips. Third, the kid-safe limits cost little quality. Keeping the flash local, capping shake at Bump strength behind a toggle, and staying under 0.12 s of hit-stop leaves every technique in this report available, because pro feel comes from contrast and timing, not intensity.
